# 6.  Fractality & The Importance of Small Changes

## Definition
The principle that complex systems and profound transformations arise from a series of small, consistent actions, rather than from grand, sweeping gestures.

## Explanation
This concept suggests that significant change doesn’t require monumental efforts but rather a series of incremental improvements, each building upon the previous one.  It’s a reminder to focus on the “square meter” – the immediate, local context – and trust that larger patterns will emerge organically.

## Key Insight
“It's a totally different state of mind.” –  Small, consistent actions, when compounded over time, can have a transformative effect.

## Examples
The speaker’s analogy of the space pen, the focus on facilitating conditions rather than imposing solutions.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Compounding]], [[Systems Thinking]]
* Opposes: [[Linear Thinking]]

## Notes
This concept highlights the power of small, consistent actions in driving long-term change.

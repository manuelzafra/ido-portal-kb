# The Trap of Narrow Performance Goals

## Definition
This concept refers to the tendency to focus exclusively on achieving specific, measurable outcomes in movement, leading to rigidity, anxiety, and a diminished capacity for genuine exploration and discovery.

## Explanation
When we fixate on a narrow performance goal – like “running faster,” “lifting heavier,” or “perfecting a specific movement” – we create a self-limiting framework that inhibits our ability to truly experience and learn movement. This focus on external validation and achievement can stifle creativity, spontaneity, and the inherent joy of movement.

## Key Insight
Genuine movement progress arises not from striving for a defined outcome, but from embracing the process of exploration and allowing the body to guide the way.

## Examples
*   A weightlifter obsessing over lifting a specific weight, leading to tension, fear of failure, and ultimately, a plateau in progress.
*   A runner fixated on achieving a certain pace, causing them to ignore their body’s signals and potentially leading to injury.

# The “Taste” – Direct Sensory Engagement

## Definition
This concept describes a mode of understanding that prioritizes direct sensory experience over intellectual analysis or conceptualization, emphasizing intuitive knowing and immediate feedback.

## Explanation
It’s about approaching a situation or skill not with a pre-determined plan or expectation, but with an open mind and a willingness to simply *be* present in the moment, allowing yourself to be guided by your senses and your intuition. It’s recognizing that true understanding often arises from direct experience, not from abstract thought.

## Key Insight
“The more you know, the less you understand.” – This highlights the limitations of intellectual knowledge and the importance of embodied experience.

## Examples
*   A musician improvising a solo, responding to the sound of the instrument and the energy of the moment, rather than adhering to a pre-composed melody.
*   A chef tasting a dish, adjusting the seasoning and ingredients based on their sensory perceptions, rather than following a recipe rigidly.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Unfolding]], [[Multiplicity]]
*   Opposes: [[Automaticity]] (where actions become habitual and detached from sensory awareness)

## Notes
This concept is central to Rido’s approach to learning and development, emphasizing the importance of cultivating a heightened state of awareness and receptivity. It’s about moving beyond simply knowing *about* something to actually *experiencing* it.

# People Are Not Made of Sugar – Embracing Discomfort

## Definition
This concept argues that we have become overly protected and “padded” by our modern lifestyles, leading to a diminished capacity for movement and a lack of resilience.

## Explanation
The speaker critiques the tendency to shield ourselves from discomfort and pain, arguing that this ultimately weakens our bodies and limits our potential. He advocates for embracing a degree of discomfort and challenge to develop strength, adaptability, and a deeper connection to our movement capabilities.

## Key Insight
“People are not made of sugar.” – This is the central assertion of the concept, highlighting the need to resist the urge to constantly protect ourselves from discomfort.

## Examples
*   The observation of children wearing excessive footwear and clothing, hindering their natural movement development.
*   The speaker’s own experience with taping up his hands during ring work, advocating for a gradual reduction in reliance on external support.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Movement Ecology]], [[Primitive Movement]], [[Neuroplasticity]]
*   Opposes: [[Overprotection]] (excessively shielding the body from all forms of stress and discomfort)

## Notes
*   This concept emphasizes the importance of challenging our comfort zones and embracing the natural challenges of movement.

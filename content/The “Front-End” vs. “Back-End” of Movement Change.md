# 6.  The “Front-End” vs. “Back-End” of Movement Change

## Definition
This concept describes a layered approach to movement change, distinguishing between the observable “front-end” (the visible movement patterns) and the underlying “back-end” (the deeper, more subtle changes in body organization and tension).

## Explanation
Changes in movement don’t simply happen at the surface level.  True movement change requires addressing the underlying structural and neurological factors that influence movement patterns.

## Key Insight
“It’s like I'm going to stand behind Nassima and I'm going to put my hands underneath his armpits and I'm going to, I'm going to tell him give me your weight and he'll not because he'll feel I can, I'm not going to support that. So he'll have a difficulty to give his weight.” – Illustrating the need for a deeper, more fundamental change.

## Examples
The speaker’s use of the rope to manipulate the body’s position is intended to influence the “back-end” of movement, rather than simply changing the visible movement pattern.

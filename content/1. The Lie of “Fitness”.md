# 1. The Lie of “Fitness”

## Definition
“Fitness” as a singular, prescriptive concept – implying a single, achievable state – is a fundamental falsehood. It’s a product of a narrow, externally-defined standard, ignoring the diverse demands of different environments and individual needs.

## Explanation
This concept highlights the core argument: “fitness” isn’t a fixed destination but a dynamic adaptation to specific conditions. It’s a reductionist term that ignores the complexity of human movement and the varied requirements of different environments.

## Key Insight
True “fitness” isn’t a state to be achieved, but the *capacity* to adapt and thrive within a given context.

## Examples
*   A marathon runner’s “fitness” is optimized for sustained aerobic exertion, while a climber’s is geared towards explosive power and agility.

# 1. The Illusion of Structure & The Scaffolding

## Definition
This concept describes the distinction between a perceived “structure” (like a routine or a rigid practice) and the underlying reality – a supportive framework that allows for fluidity and adaptation.

## Explanation
The core idea is that rigidly imposed structures can become a barrier to genuine movement and learning. They’re “scaffolding” – useful initially, but ultimately limiting if they don’t evolve with understanding.  The yogis recognized this, moving beyond simply replicating postures to understanding the *transition* and the underlying principles.

## Key Insight
True movement isn’t about adhering to a fixed structure; it’s about building a resilient framework that adapts to the ever-changing demands of the environment.

## Examples
*   “It is not the actual thing. It is the scaffolding, not the building.” – Speaker 1
*   The analogy of a rigid routine versus a flowing, improvisational approach.

# 4. The “Unlearning” of Assumptions

## Definition
This concept describes the process of actively questioning and discarding ingrained assumptions and habitual ways of thinking, particularly within the context of learning and skill acquisition.

## Explanation
It’s about recognizing that our existing frameworks can limit our ability to truly understand and engage with new information. This requires a willingness to challenge our own beliefs and embrace alternative perspectives.

## Key Insight
“It’s like people make mistakes, they're not finished products. And you must be able to also see the other person and their struggles to become and actually share the journey and then chemical sexual compatibility, and then these together, boom, this is the highest chances you can have, versus all kinds of other factors which are, you know, some kind of a romantic emotional compatibility, exactly not, like opposites actually attracting that regard many times.”

## Examples
The speaker’s willingness to “turn and go somewhere else” when encountering a closed-off teacher or a negative attitude exemplifies this approach.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Cognitive Flexibility]], [[Metacognition]]
*   Opposes: [[Confirmation Bias]]

## Notes
This highlights the importance of critical thinking and a willingness to adapt one’s approach to learning.

# 3. The Beginner’s Mind (Shoshin)

## Definition
The “beginner’s mind” (Shoshin) is a state of openness, eagerness, and humility, characterized by a willingness to accept mistakes and embrace the unfamiliar.

## Explanation
Ido emphasizes the importance of approaching movement with the mindset of a beginner, even after years of experience. This involves letting go of preconceived notions, accepting imperfection, and focusing on the process of learning rather than achieving a specific outcome.

## Key Insight
Embracing a beginner’s mind is crucial for unlocking exponential learning and adaptability in movement practice.

## Examples
Ido’s repeated instruction to “keep going, keep going, keep going” during the one-arm handstand training exemplifies this concept – pushing through discomfort and accepting failure as part of the learning process.

## Notes
This concept is central to Ido’s methodology, highlighting the importance of embracing vulnerability and accepting the inherent challenges of learning new skills.

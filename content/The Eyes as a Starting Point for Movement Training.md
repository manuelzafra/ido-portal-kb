# 2. The Eyes as a Starting Point for Movement Training

## Definition
Utilizing the eyes as the initial point of focus during movement practice to influence and calibrate the nervous system, impacting subsequent movement patterns.

## Explanation
This suggests that addressing visual input – how we see and how we move our eyes – can have a profound ripple effect on the entire movement system, influencing posture, balance, and coordination. It’s a “reset” mechanism for the body.

## Key Insight
Modulating visual input early in a movement practice can significantly influence subsequent movement execution.

## Examples
*   The speaker’s instruction to pull the head to the side to immediately organize the feet, demonstrating a direct link between visual input and postural control.
*   The observation that myopia (nearsightedness) is linked to a lack of panoramic vision.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Neuromuscular Control]], [[Sensory Integration]]
*   Opposes: [[Blindly Following Instinct]]

## Notes
This highlights the importance of the eyes as a primary sensory input channel and its potential to directly shape movement.

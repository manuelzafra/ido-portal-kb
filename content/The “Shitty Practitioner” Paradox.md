# The “Shitty Practitioner” Paradox

## Definition
This concept highlights the crucial link between dissatisfaction and growth in a practice. A practitioner who is *satisfied* with their current level of performance is, by definition, stagnant and not pushing themselves to evolve.

## Explanation
It’s a provocative statement designed to challenge complacency. The “shitty practitioner” is the one who is content with mediocrity, who doesn’t actively seek out challenges or strive for improvement. It’s a call to embrace discomfort and actively seek out areas for growth.

## Key Insight
True progress requires a willingness to be uncomfortable and to acknowledge one’s own limitations.

## Examples
*   A weightlifter who consistently achieves a certain weight without pushing themselves to increase it is a “shitty practitioner.”
*   A martial artist who relies on pre-programmed techniques without actively seeking to develop their own movement vocabulary is a “shitty practitioner.”

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Progressive Overload]] (the need to constantly challenge oneself)
*   Opposes: [[Cartesian State of Mind]] (the belief that mastery is achieved through knowledge alone)

## Notes
This concept is a powerful motivator – it’s a reminder that growth is not about achieving a state of perfection, but about continuously striving to improve.

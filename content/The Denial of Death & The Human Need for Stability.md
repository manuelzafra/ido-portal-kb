# The Denial of Death & The Human Need for Stability

## Definition
This concept describes the human tendency to create symbolic representations of death – such as memorials – as a way to grapple with the inevitability of mortality and to establish a sense of control over the unknown.

## Explanation
The speaker observes that humans often erect monuments and memorials to honor those who died in war, suggesting this is a subconscious attempt to confront the fear of death. These memorials provide a framework for understanding death, offering a narrative that can be comforting, even if ultimately illusory.

## Key Insight
“And I think he was talking specifically about 1940 when it was the Royal Air Force defending Britain from a pending German invasion.” – This highlights the specific historical context driving the concept of “denial of death.”

## Examples
*   The Royal Air Force memorial in London, a physical representation of a collective attempt to grapple with death.

# Layered Sensory Awareness in Movement

## Definition
Movement practice isn’t about achieving a specific outcome (strength, flexibility, etc.) but about cultivating awareness of the body’s constant state of flux – the movement of the body, mind, and emotions – through open, decentralized engagement.

## Explanation
This concept emphasizes a shift away from goal-oriented movement towards a state of heightened awareness of the body’s inherent dynamism. It’s about recognizing that movement isn’t a static performance but a continuous, flowing process, and that this awareness itself is the primary benefit of the practice. It’s about letting go of the need to control and instead, embracing the natural movement of the body.

## Key Insight
True movement practice is the ongoing examination and awareness of the body’s constant state of motion, not the attainment of a particular physical outcome.

## Examples
Ido’s observation of injecting playfulness during a walk, noticing the subtle shifts in posture and awareness, and his deliberate exploration of different distances from others.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Magnocellar Pathway]], [[Peripersonal Space]], [[Neuroplasticity]]
* Opposes: [[Fixed Postures]], [[Overly Focused Technique]]

## Notes
This concept aligns with the idea of embodied cognition – the understanding that cognition isn’t solely a brain-based process but is deeply intertwined with the body’s sensory experience.

# Meditating on Mortality for Appreciative Living

## Definition
A daily practice of contemplating one’s own mortality to cultivate a deeper appreciation for the present moment and to accept impermanence.

## Explanation
This involves intentionally reflecting on the transient nature of life, not as a morbid exercise, but as a tool for prioritizing experiences and fostering gratitude for the present. It’s rooted in ancient practices like those found in samurai culture.

## Key Insight
Acknowledging the potential for cessation of existence can dramatically increase the value and intensity of lived experience.

## Examples
Regularly considering “what if this is the last time I…” when engaging in an activity, or simply pausing to reflect on the impermanence of all things.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Viktor Frankl’s Logotherapy]], [[Stoic Philosophy]]
* Opposes: [[Denial of Mortality]]

## Notes
This practice is about shifting perspective, not generating fear, but fostering a sense of urgency and appreciation.

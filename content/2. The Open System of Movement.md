# 2. The Open System of Movement

## Definition
Movement is inherently decentralized, lacking a fixed center of control, and can be approached from any point of reference, making it a fundamentally open system.

## Explanation
This describes movement as a dynamic, non-hierarchical process. It rejects the notion of a central “command center” in the body and emphasizes the interconnectedness of all parts. It’s about embracing the fluidity and unpredictability of movement.

## Key Insight
Movement is a decentralized, open system, allowing for a wider range of exploration and adaptation.

## Examples
*   Ido’s description of movement as “having no center.”
*   The ability to approach movement from any point of reference – body, playfulness, self-inquiry.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Emergence]], [[Decentralization]]
*   Opposes: [[Centralized Control]]

## Notes
This concept is crucial for understanding why movement feels so intuitive and adaptable.

# The Importance of Ground Contact

## Definition
The deliberate and frequent engagement of the body with the ground as a fundamental element of movement training and development.

## Explanation
This concept emphasizes the significance of maintaining contact with the ground – through crawling, balancing, or other tactile movements – as a way to stimulate proprioception, improve coordination, and foster a deeper connection with the body’s movement capabilities.

## Key Insight
“People don't touch the floor anymore… People simply don't make the simplest contact with the ground.” –  Highlighting the loss of this fundamental sensory input in modern movement practices.

## Examples
*   Crawling on the floor like a lizard, deliberately engaging the sense of touch and proprioception to improve mobility and coordination.
*   Using a broomstick to explore different planes of motion, focusing on tactile feedback and balance adjustments.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Sensory Integration]], [[Proprioception]]
*   Opposes: [[Elevated Movement Practices]]

## Notes
This concept underscores the importance of returning to basic movement principles and re-establishing a fundamental connection with the ground.

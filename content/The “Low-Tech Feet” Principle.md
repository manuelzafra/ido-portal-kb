# The “Low-Tech Feet” Principle

## Definition
A movement philosophy prioritizing the inherent capabilities of the human body and feet, minimizing reliance on external aids and technology.

## Explanation
This principle suggests that the most effective movement training stems from developing a deep understanding and connection with the body’s natural movement patterns, utilizing basic tools and techniques rather than expensive, technologically-advanced equipment.

## Key Insight
“High-tech shoes, low-tech feet.” – Recognizing that sophisticated footwear can actually hinder natural movement capabilities.

## Examples
*   Using a broomstick as a primary training tool, rather than relying on specialized equipment.
*   Focusing on fundamental movement skills like crawling and balancing, without the assistance of supportive devices.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Sensory Feedback]], [[Biofeedback]]
*   Opposes: [[Over-Reliance on Technology in Movement]]

## Notes
This concept underscores the importance of respecting the body’s natural biomechanics and avoiding the detrimental effects of overly restrictive or supportive equipment.

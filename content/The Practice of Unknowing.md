# The Practice of Unknowing

## Definition
A mode of practice rooted in recognizing fundamental limitations and needs, utilizing texts like Borges as tools for exploration rather than adhering to established movement or cultural frameworks.

## Explanation
This concept centers on deliberately stepping outside conventional approaches to learning and movement. It’s about acknowledging what we *cannot* know or achieve, and employing methods – like engaging with dense literature – to navigate that unknowing, fostering a deeper, more personal understanding.

## Key Insight
True progress lies in embracing limitations and using them as a catalyst for a unique, self-directed practice.

## Examples
The use of Borges’ works as a “practice” – a deliberate, focused engagement designed to unlock insights beyond simple narrative consumption.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Layered Sensory Awareness in Movement]], [[The Importance of Constraints]]
* Opposes: [[Collector Mentality]] (the tendency to rigidly adhere to established systems or trends)

## Notes
This concept highlights the danger of becoming overly defined by movement culture and the value of cultivating a space for genuine, unburdened exploration.

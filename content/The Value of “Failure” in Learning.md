# 2. The Value of “Failure” in Learning

## Definition
The deliberate introduction of errors and mistakes into a training process as a crucial mechanism for identifying weaknesses, refining technique, and building resilience.

## Explanation
This concept challenges the traditional aversion to failure, arguing that it’s a necessary component of skill acquisition. By actively seeking out and analyzing errors, learners can gain a deeper understanding of their movement limitations and develop strategies for overcoming them.

## Key Insight
“Learning through negation” – actively creating failures to map the boundaries of what is possible.

## Examples
*   “I couldn't imagine how people teach Olympic lifts without ever teaching to drop the weight.” –  Dropping the weight is a controlled failure that reveals fundamental weaknesses in technique.
*   “Learning through negation, I call it. First, the failure is experienced, then we can go for the actual performance.” – This highlights the sequential nature of this learning process.

# Layered Labyrinths

## Definition
This concept describes a progression through increasingly complex problems, starting with smaller puzzles and escalating to large, multi-faceted challenges. The key is recognizing the transition between these levels.

## Explanation
The idea is that learning and problem-solving isn't linear. Individuals initially encounter simpler situations (small puzzles) before confronting larger, more intricate problems. Successfully navigating each level leads to a new, more complex one.

## Key Insight
Progress in learning and problem-solving involves deliberately moving from manageable challenges to increasingly complex ones, recognizing the shift in scale.

## Examples
A student learning to code might start with simple "Hello, World!" programs before tackling a complex web application.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Adaptive Learning]], [[Complex Systems]]
* Opposes: [[Fixed Mindset]] (suggests resisting complexity)

## Notes
The “middle” represents a point of clarity and strategic thinking after navigating a smaller challenge. It’s a moment of consolidation before tackling the next level.

# The Value of Questions Over Answers

## Definition
The prioritization of inquiry and exploration over definitive solutions. True understanding emerges not from possessing answers, but from continually asking questions and challenging assumptions.

## Explanation
Portell’s emphasis on a culture that “appreciates the questions, not the answers” reveals a profound epistemology. He suggests that answers are fleeting and often incomplete, while questions are the engines of progress. This aligns with the Socratic method, which uses questioning to expose contradictions and stimulate critical thinking. By focusing on questions, we remain open to new information and perspectives, avoiding the trap of premature closure. This approach is particularly valuable in fields like movement, where the body is constantly revealing new complexities and challenging established paradigms. The act of questioning fosters humility and a recognition that our understanding is always provisional.

## Key Insight
True understanding is not found in possessing answers, but in continually asking questions and challenging assumptions.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Instead of seeking the "perfect" squat form, asking "What is the purpose of this movement?" and "How can I adapt it to my individual needs?"
* **Life:** Instead of accepting conventional wisdom, asking "Why do I believe this?" and "What evidence supports this claim?"

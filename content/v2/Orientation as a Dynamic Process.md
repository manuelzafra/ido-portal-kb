# Orientation as a Dynamic Process

## Definition
This concept proposes that finding one's "center" or orientation is not a fixed state but an ongoing process of recalibration within a constantly evolving context. It moves beyond a static sense of self to a dynamic adaptation to new challenges.

## Explanation
The speaker’s suggestion to “find your center and then work out what to do from there” implies that this center isn’t a permanent anchor. The subsequent revelation of moving from one labyrinth to a larger one immediately undermines the notion of a stable orientation. Instead, it suggests that finding one’s center is a temporary act of re-alignment, a necessary step before encountering a new, more complex set of challenges. This dynamic view of orientation necessitates a constant willingness to re-evaluate one's position and adjust one’s course, implying a flexible and adaptable mindset. It’s not about *having* a center, but about the *process* of finding it repeatedly.

## Key Insight
True orientation isn’t a fixed point, but a continuous process of recalibration in response to ever-changing circumstances.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A dancer adjusting their balance and intention after a slight shift in momentum.
* **Life:** A professional re-evaluating their career goals after a significant industry change.

## Notes
This concept can be challenging to grasp because it requires letting go of the desire for a stable sense of self and embracing the inherent instability of existence. It's a call to cultivate a mindset of continuous adaptation and learning.

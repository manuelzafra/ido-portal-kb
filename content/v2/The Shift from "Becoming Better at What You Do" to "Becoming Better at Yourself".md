# The Shift from "Becoming Better at What You Do" to "Becoming Better at Yourself"

## Definition
A profound shift in focus from striving for external achievement and mastery in a specific skill to prioritizing personal growth, self-understanding, and continuous self-evolution.

## Explanation
Portell describes a pivotal moment in his journey where he realized the limitations of pursuing external goals. He moved away from striving to "become better at what I do" (e.g., a specific movement practice) and instead focused on "becoming better at myself." This represents a fundamental shift in values, from external validation to internal development. It's a move away from a performance-oriented mindset towards a process-oriented one. This resonates with existentialist philosophy, which emphasizes individual responsibility and the pursuit of authentic selfhood. The tools and practices become secondary to the underlying goal of self-discovery and personal evolution. This concept is particularly relevant in a culture that often equates worth with achievement.

## Key Insight
True fulfillment lies not in mastering external skills, but in cultivating personal growth and self-understanding.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Focusing on improving body awareness and movement quality rather than simply achieving a specific performance metric.
* **Life:** Prioritizing emotional intelligence and self-reflection over career advancement.

## Notes
This shift requires a willingness to confront uncomfortable truths about oneself and to prioritize long-term personal growth over short-term external gains.

# The Pursuit of Becoming as a Guiding Principle

## Definition
This concept centers on the idea that personal growth isn't about defining who one *is* but rather about actively pursuing who one *wants to become*. It emphasizes a future-oriented mindset, prioritizing aspiration and potential over current limitations.

## Explanation
Speaker 00’s repeated emphasis on "becoming" rather than "being" reveals a dynamic and aspirational philosophy. It's not about accepting a fixed self-image but about embracing a continuous process of evolution. This perspective transcends self-acceptance, suggesting that even perceived flaws ("I'm weak, I'm afraid") are not defining characteristics but rather starting points for transformation. This aligns with a growth mindset, where challenges are seen as opportunities for development, and effort is valued over innate ability. The speaker's orientation towards a future self—"who do I want to be"—provides a compass for navigating life's complexities and motivates action. It's a rejection of stagnation in favor of a perpetual journey of self-discovery.

## Key Insight
True personal growth is not about accepting who you are, but about relentlessly pursuing the person you aspire to be.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "who who I am today I'm weak I'm you know I'm afraid I have this I have that I'm I'm old I'm you know all these ideas that's who I am but who do I want to be I want to be that person who doesn't mind this this and that"
* **Outside Example:** An athlete might acknowledge their current weaknesses (e.g., lack of speed) but focus their training on developing those qualities, rather than dwelling on their limitations.

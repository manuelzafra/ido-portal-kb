# The Importance of Questioning Over Answers

## Definition
The prioritization of inquiry and exploration over definitive solutions. The process of asking questions, even if they lead to further uncertainty, is more valuable than arriving at a seemingly conclusive answer.

## Explanation
Portell emphasizes that his culture "appreciates the questions, not the answers so much." This isn't a mere preference; it's a fundamental guiding principle. He frames the pursuit of knowledge as a continuous process of questioning, a "living inside the question." Solutions, he argues, are transient and may even disappear. The true value lies in the ongoing investigation itself. This perspective aligns with Socratic method, which prioritizes questioning assumptions and challenging conventional wisdom. It also suggests a distrust of dogma and a commitment to intellectual humility.

## Key Insight
The most valuable pursuit is not the attainment of answers, but the relentless questioning that drives exploration and understanding.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "I come from a culture and a place that appreciates the questions, not the answers so much. And I think that direction of asking the questions together and asking you questions and asking myself questions is more important than the verdict, the solution."
* **Outside Movement:** A scientist who, instead of declaring a definitive theory, poses a series of questions to guide further research.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Socratic Method]], [[Intellectual Humility]]

## Notes
This concept can be misinterpreted as a rejection of expertise. Portell isn't suggesting that answers are irrelevant, but rather that the process of questioning is paramount.

# The Value of "Wordlessness" in Experiential Learning

## Definition
The concept that non-verbal experiences and direct sensory engagement are often more effective than verbal instruction in fostering genuine understanding and embodied knowledge.

## Explanation
Portal advocates for "non-verbal experiences" as a powerful entry point into movement practice, suggesting that language can often be a barrier to true understanding. This aligns with the idea that much of our knowledge is tacit – that is, it's embodied and difficult to articulate. Verbal explanations can impose conceptual frameworks that limit exploration and prevent the discovery of deeper insights. By prioritizing direct sensory experience, individuals can bypass the limitations of language and access a more intuitive and embodied form of knowledge. This principle extends beyond movement, suggesting that experiential learning – whether through art, music, or even scientific inquiry – is often more valuable than purely theoretical instruction. It underscores the importance of "skin in the game," of actively engaging with the world through our senses and bodies.

## Key Insight
True understanding often arises not from what is said, but from what is directly experienced and felt.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Learning a dance by imitation rather than through verbal instruction.
* **Learning:** Experiencing a scientific phenomenon firsthand rather than simply reading about it in a textbook.
* **Relationships:** Communicating through non-verbal cues and shared experiences rather than relying solely on verbal communication.

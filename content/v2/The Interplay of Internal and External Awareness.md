# The Interplay of Internal and External Awareness

## Definition
The recognition that movement practice involves a constant negotiation between the individual's internal sensations and external stimuli, a process crucial for self-awareness and differentiation.

## Explanation
Moshe Feldenkrais’s framework, as articulated by Ido Portal, highlights the body's core function: differentiating between "me" and "not me." Movement practice, therefore, isn’t just about physical action; it’s about feeling the external forces acting upon the body *and* the internal movements arising within. This dual awareness is fundamental to understanding one’s own embodiment.  This concept has implications for emotional regulation and social interaction, as it suggests that our sense of self is constantly shaped by the interplay between internal states and external feedback.  The ability to discern these layers is "tricky" and "elusive," requiring dedicated practice and a willingness to observe subtle sensations.

## Key Insight
True embodiment arises from the ability to consciously differentiate between internal sensations and external stimuli, fostering a deeper understanding of self.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "And I think when you feel movement, you feel the movement of the outside that is, of course, arriving to you and receiving this and also your own internal movement."
* **Outside Example:** A dancer learning a new choreography. They must not only execute the steps but also feel how their body responds to the space, the music, and the other dancers.

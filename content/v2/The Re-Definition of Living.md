# The Re-Definition of Living

## Definition
This provocative concept challenges the conventional understanding of "living" as a positive and forward-moving process, proposing instead that it is fundamentally a process of "dying" – a continuous unfolding towards inevitable end.

## Explanation
The speaker radically redefines "living" as "dying," arguing that the conventional term obscures the inherent reality of our existence.  He suggests that "living" implies a trajectory of growth and progress that doesn't accurately reflect the reality of decay and decline. By reframing life as a process of dying, we can confront the impermanence of existence and find a deeper appreciation for the present moment. This perspective encourages a shift in focus from striving for an elusive future to embracing the beauty and wisdom that arise from acknowledging our mortality.  It’s a philosophical provocation designed to disrupt ingrained assumptions and foster a more honest and authentic engagement with life.

## Key Insight
Acknowledging that life is a process of dying can unlock a profound sense of presence and appreciation for the fleeting nature of existence.

## Examples
* **Textual:** Fred’s direct statement that “we are dying here.”
* **Outside:** A hospice worker who witnesses the end-of-life journey firsthand may develop a deeper understanding of the beauty and meaning of life.

## Notes
This is a deliberately provocative statement intended to challenge conventional thinking. It's not meant to be taken literally as a denial of vitality, but as a reminder of the inevitability of death and the importance of living fully in the present.

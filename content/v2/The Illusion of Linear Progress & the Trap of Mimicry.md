# The Illusion of Linear Progress & the Trap of Mimicry

## Definition
The tendency to assume that achieving a desired outcome (e.g., a deep squat, a heavy bench press) requires a linear progression and replicating the methods of those who have already achieved it, overlooking individual differences and underlying limitations. This creates a framework where individuals feel inadequate if they don't match a perceived standard, hindering genuine progress.

## Explanation
The speakers critique the common approach of presenting advanced movement patterns or strength feats as achievable through a simple, linear progression. They highlight the danger of assuming everyone possesses the same physiological foundation or has followed a similar path. This creates a "trap of mimicry," where individuals feel discouraged when they can't immediately replicate what they see, leading to frustration and potential injury. The analogy of the 500-pound bench press illustrates this perfectly: suggesting someone can reach that level by simply adding a small amount of weight and repeating sets ignores the years of dedicated training and individual variation involved. This framework implicitly promotes a hierarchical view of ability, where those who haven't reached a certain level are seen as "behind," rather than recognizing that everyone's journey is unique. The speakers advocate for a more nuanced approach that acknowledges individual differences and focuses on personalized investigation rather than prescribed routines.

## Key Insight
True progress isn't about replicating someone else's journey, but about understanding your own limitations and embarking on a personalized investigation of movement and strength.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "I've seen a lot of that in the mobility arena. And it would be equivalent of me saying, oh, you want to bench 500 pounds, here's how you do it. You just do 455 for a couple sets of three, five sets of three, and you progressively do that over the course of a few weeks, and that's a 500 pound bench very easily."
* **Outside Movement:** In education, this manifests as expecting all students to learn at the same pace or through the same methods, ignoring different learning styles and prior knowledge. In relationships, it's the expectation that all successful partnerships follow a specific formula, disregarding individual needs and communication styles.

## Notes
This concept is easily misunderstood because it challenges the common narrative of "following a proven system" for achieving goals. The speakers aren't advocating against structured training, but against the rigid application of methods without considering individual context.

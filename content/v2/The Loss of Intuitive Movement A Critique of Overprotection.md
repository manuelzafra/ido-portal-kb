# The Loss of Intuitive Movement: A Critique of Overprotection

## Definition
This concept critiques the modern tendency to overprotect children, leading to a loss of natural, intuitive movement patterns and ultimately hindering their physical development. It argues that excessive padding and restrictions stifle the body's innate ability to adapt and learn.

## Explanation
The speaker's anecdote about the child whose feet hurt on grass highlights a concerning trend: the erosion of children's natural movement capabilities due to overprotection. By shielding children from discomfort and challenges, parents inadvertently deprive them of the opportunity to develop resilience and adaptability. The speaker laments the loss of the "W position" on the floor, a natural posture that contributes to flexibility and mobility, and contrasts it with the restrictive nature of prolonged chair sitting. This framework extends beyond physical health, suggesting that overprotection can also hinder emotional and psychological development. The speaker's frustration is rooted in the belief that children possess an innate understanding of their bodies and a natural capacity for movement that should be nurtured, not suppressed.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of safety and comfort can inadvertently undermine the very qualities – resilience, adaptability, and physical competence – that protect us.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A child being discouraged from climbing trees or playing rough-and-tumble games.
* **Outside Movement:** A society that prioritizes safety and risk aversion over exploration and experimentation.

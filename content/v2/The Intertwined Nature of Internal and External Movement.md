# The Intertwined Nature of Internal and External Movement

## Definition
The understanding that our perception of movement is a complex interplay between the sensations arising from our own internal processes and the external stimuli we receive from the environment.  Differentiating between these two is crucial for developing self-awareness and refining movement skills.

## Explanation
Moshe Feldenkrais's framework, as described by Ido Portal, highlights the body's core function as a receiver of both internal and external information.  Early childhood development involves a crucial process of distinguishing "me" from "not me," and this distinction is fundamentally rooted in the experience of movement and stillness. Recognizing this interplay allows practitioners to move beyond superficial techniques and delve into the deeper mechanics of embodiment. It’s not just about *doing* a movement, but about *feeling* the interaction between your body and the world around you, and your internal state. This concept has implications for understanding proprioception, interoception, and the embodied cognition.

## Key Insight
True movement mastery requires a deep awareness of the reciprocal relationship between our internal states and the external world, fostering a nuanced understanding of self and environment.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A dancer noticing how their posture shifts in response to the music and the energy of the audience.
* **Emotional Regulation:** Recognizing that physical tension (e.g., clenched jaw) is often a manifestation of underlying emotional stress.
* **Social Interaction:** Becoming aware of how your body language and posture influence the dynamics of a conversation.

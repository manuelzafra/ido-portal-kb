# The Cyclical Nature of Practice and the Inevitable Return to Simplicity

## Definition
The idea that even after periods of intense growth and complexity in any practice (movement, art, life), individuals inevitably gravitate back towards simpler, more familiar approaches, not necessarily as a regression, but as a necessary phase for reflection, integration, and eventual renewed advancement.

## Explanation
Ido's assertion that "If you're satisfied about your practice, you're a shitty practitioner" and his subsequent discussion about returning to simpler practices after periods of intense complexity highlights a cyclical model of growth. It suggests that pushing boundaries and exploring new frontiers are essential, but that periods of perceived "regression" – returning to familiar techniques or simpler forms – are not failures, but vital for consolidation and preventing burnout. This isn't a linear progression; it’s a spiral, where one revisits earlier stages with new understanding. The analogy of Sisyphus, endlessly pushing a boulder uphill, encapsulates this cycle – the effort is constant, and progress isn't always visible. This framework resonates with the concept of "productive nostalgia" – revisiting the past not to dwell on it, but to extract new insights and inform future directions.

## Key Insight
The perceived "regression" in practice is not a failure, but a necessary phase of integration and renewal within a cyclical process of growth.

## Examples
* **Textual:** Ido’s discussion of returning to simpler practices after periods of intense complexity.
* **Outside:** A musician revisiting basic scales after mastering complex compositions, a writer returning to journaling after publishing a novel, a businessperson simplifying processes after implementing complex strategies.

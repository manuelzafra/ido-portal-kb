# Learning Through Negation: Defining Skill Boundaries

## Definition
This concept describes a pedagogical approach where failure and error are intentionally introduced as a crucial step in learning a skill. By experiencing what *doesn't* work, the learner defines the boundaries of the skill and gains a deeper understanding of the correct execution.

## Explanation
The speaker explicitly states, "Learning through negation, I call it." This isn't about avoiding failure, but actively seeking it out as a learning tool. By experiencing the consequences of a failed attempt (e.g., falling from a box, dropping a weight), the learner gains a visceral understanding of the limits of their ability and the necessary adjustments for success. This contrasts with a purely positive reinforcement approach, which can lead to a superficial understanding of the skill. The analogy of mapping the "correct skill by marking the boundaries of what is failures all around it" is particularly insightful. This method isn't just about learning *what* to do, but *why* it works.

## Key Insight
True skill acquisition requires embracing failure as a vital source of information, allowing learners to define the boundaries of competence through direct experience.

## Examples
* **Text:** The speaker's description of teaching break falling before teaching acrobatics.
* **Outside:** A software developer learning a new programming language by intentionally introducing bugs and observing their effects.

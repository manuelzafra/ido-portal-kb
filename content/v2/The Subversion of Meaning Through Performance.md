# The Subversion of Meaning Through Performance

## Definition
The act of creating a persona or performance, even with good intentions, can ultimately dilute the authenticity of one's beliefs and actions, leading to a sense of inauthenticity and a need for constant external validation.

## Explanation
The speaker's discussion of his approach to podcasting reveals a nuanced understanding of how even seemingly positive actions can be corrupted by the desire for external approval. He acknowledges manipulating the platform and audience to spread his ideas, but also recognizes the inherent risk of sacrificing authenticity in the process. This is not simply about seeking fame; it's about the subtle shift in motivation that occurs when actions are performed for an audience rather than for oneself. The speaker’s aversion to “Conor McGregor-style” sensationalism highlights a fear of sacrificing integrity for the sake of viral attention. This concept aligns with Erving Goffman's work on the presentation of self in everyday life, where individuals constantly manage impressions to fit social expectations.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of meaning through external validation can paradoxically undermine the very authenticity that makes it valuable.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "I manipulate you. You manipulate me and I manipulate you. I give you certain things which are beneficial for your podcast and development and as a human being as well. And I manipulate you through this channel of attention and exposure to get these ideas out there, not for my financial gain."
* **Outside Example:** A social media influencer who promotes a lifestyle they don’t genuinely live, creating a disconnect between their online persona and their true self.

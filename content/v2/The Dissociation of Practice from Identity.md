# The Dissociation of Practice from Identity

## Definition
This concept describes a deliberate rejection of professional or cultural boundaries in favor of pursuing a deeper, undefined purpose. It involves actively choosing to incorporate elements from seemingly unrelated fields (literature, philosophy) into one's practice, even when it contradicts expectations or diminishes audience size.

## Explanation
The speaker actively resists being defined by his profession ("movement teacher," "fitness practitioner," "developer of movement culture"). This isn't mere rebellion; it's a strategic decoupling of practice from identity. He understands that adherence to established norms and expectations limits the potential for genuine exploration and growth. By freeing himself from the constraints of "movement culture," he can draw from diverse sources – Borges, Dostoevsky – not as stylistic flourishes, but as tools for a deeper, unarticulated practice. This dissociation allows him to serve a "higher thing" that transcends the limitations of any specific discipline, even the concept of "practice" itself. The shrinking audience is a necessary consequence of this pursuit, a testament to the increasing specialization required to grasp the underlying principles.

## Key Insight
True growth necessitates a willingness to abandon established identities and embrace practices that defy conventional categorization.

## Examples
* **Textual:** The speaker’s incorporation of Borges and Dostoevsky into movement practice, despite it being considered unusual by many in the field.
* **Outside Movement:** A scientist who draws on art and music to develop new theories, or a lawyer who incorporates philosophy to approach cases with greater nuance and empathy.

## Notes
This concept is easily misunderstood as mere eccentricity. The speaker’s intention is not to be different for the sake of difference, but to access a deeper level of understanding that is obscured by professional boundaries.

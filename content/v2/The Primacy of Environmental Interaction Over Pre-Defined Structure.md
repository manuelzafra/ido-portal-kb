# The Primacy of Environmental Interaction Over Pre-Defined Structure

## Definition
True learning and development are driven not by adherence to pre-determined structures (like agility ladders or rigid training programs), but by dynamic interaction with a constantly changing environment. This interaction fosters adaptability, creativity, and a deeper understanding of movement principles.

## Explanation
The speaker contrasts the rigidity of structures like agility ladders with the organic nature of children’s games and natural environments. The agility ladder, while offering a defined pattern, quickly becomes predictable and loses its novelty. In contrast, navigating a forest floor or playing "stepping on feet" demands constant adaptation and improvisation. This highlights a fundamental principle: learning isn't about mastering a system, but about mastering the ability to respond to the unexpected. This aligns with ecological approaches to learning, which emphasize the interconnectedness of the individual and their environment. The speaker’s experiences with the Indonesian tribe further illustrate this point – their bodies adapted to the uneven terrain, demonstrating the power of environmental shaping.

## Key Insight
Adaptability and true learning are cultivated through dynamic interaction with a constantly changing environment, not through adherence to pre-defined structures.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "The agility ladder always looks the same. Yeah, I can change the patterns, but I get used to that look, that thing."
* **Outside Movement:** A musician who only practices scales in a specific order will not develop the improvisational skills needed to play jazz.

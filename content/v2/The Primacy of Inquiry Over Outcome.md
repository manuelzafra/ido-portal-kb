# The Primacy of Inquiry Over Outcome

## Definition
This concept asserts that the value of a practice, particularly a movement practice, lies not in achieving a predetermined outcome or feeling a specific sensation, but in the process of rigorous, unbiased inquiry itself. It prioritizes the cultivation of a questioning mindset over the attainment of a desired result.

## Explanation
The speaker critiques the modern tendency to approach practices like gratitude or even yoga with a focus on achieving a specific feeling or outcome. He contrasts this with a deeper investigation, emphasizing that the "joke is on us" when we pursue goals without understanding the underlying forces at play. The true benefit arises from the *examination* itself, a playful and researcher-like approach where one doesn't impose pre-existing beliefs but remains open to unexpected discoveries. This echoes a broader philosophical stance – a rejection of instrumental rationality where practices are solely valued for their utility, and an embrace of epistemic humility, recognizing the limits of our current understanding. The willingness to receive "weird looks" is a crucial indicator of pushing beyond conventional boundaries and venturing into genuinely new territory.

## Key Insight
The most profound benefits of any practice are revealed not through achieving a desired result, but through the unwavering commitment to questioning and exploring the process itself.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "I have nothing that I can just sprinkle now some magic powder that will help resolve this, because it's a start of a deep investigation."
* **Outside Movement:** A scientist conducting an experiment should prioritize meticulous observation and unbiased data collection over confirming a pre-existing hypothesis. A writer should focus on exploring a theme honestly, even if it leads to unexpected conclusions, rather than forcing the narrative to fit a predetermined plot.

## Notes
This concept is easily misunderstood as advocating for aimlessness. The speaker isn't suggesting abandoning goals, but rather shifting the *primary* focus to the process of inquiry, recognizing that outcomes are often secondary and potentially misleading.

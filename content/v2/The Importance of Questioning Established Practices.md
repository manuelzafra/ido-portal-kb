# The Importance of Questioning Established Practices

## Definition
The principle that even widely accepted and seemingly beneficial practices should be continually questioned and re-evaluated, as they can become rigid dogma that hinders progress. True innovation requires a willingness to challenge the status quo and experiment with alternative approaches.

## Explanation
Portell repeatedly emphasizes the need to question established practices, even those he himself has popularized. He cites the example of the "flat foot squat," initially promoted by him, which later faced criticism and adaptation. This isn't a sign of failure, but rather a demonstration of intellectual honesty and a commitment to ongoing investigation. The danger lies in treating these practices as immutable rules, leading to stagnation and potential harm. Portell's approach aligns with a scientific mindset that prioritizes empirical evidence and continuous refinement over blind adherence to tradition. This framework encourages a culture of experimentation, where failure is viewed as a learning opportunity and innovation is driven by a relentless pursuit of improvement.

## Key Insight
Progress is not achieved by adhering to established practices, but by continually questioning and re-evaluating them.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "The flat foot squat is very useful to develop strength, but even that is not for everyone. Some people just got to work with split squatting, and they'll never really benefit from squatting. They'll only get injured from it."
* **Outside Movement:** A chef who is unwilling to experiment with new ingredients or techniques will likely fall behind in a rapidly evolving culinary landscape.

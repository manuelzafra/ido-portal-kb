# The Subversion of Static Concepts Through Experiential Framing

## Definition
This concept describes the process by which a particular way of framing experiences, especially through practices like movement, can challenge and dismantle pre-existing, rigid beliefs and assumptions about oneself and the world. It’s about disrupting ingrained perspectives and opening space for new understanding.

## Explanation
Speaker 00 explicitly states that Ido’s (Speaker 01) framing of life forces listeners to "reexamine a lot of our static concepts." This isn’t about presenting new information; it’s about altering the lens through which existing information is perceived. The power lies in the *way* things are presented, the context provided, and the invitation to engage with the material experientially. This aligns with constructivist learning theory, where knowledge isn't passively received but actively constructed through experience. It also echoes philosophical traditions that emphasize the importance of perspective and the limitations of fixed categories. The act of movement, as a recurring and embodied experience, becomes a catalyst for this re-evaluation.

## Key Insight
The most profound learning often arises not from acquiring new facts, but from experiencing phenomena in a way that challenges our pre-existing frameworks.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Experiencing a challenging yoga pose that forces a re-evaluation of one's physical limitations.
* **Outside Movement:** Reading a historical account with a critical lens, challenging previously held nationalistic beliefs.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Constructivism]], [[Critical Thinking]]
* Opposes: [[Dogmatism]] (the acceptance of beliefs without critical examination)
* Deeper pattern: [[Epistemological Humility]] (recognizing the limits of one's own knowledge)

## Notes
This concept highlights the importance of mentorship and guidance in facilitating this process of conceptual subversion. It's not always possible to dismantle static concepts on one's own.

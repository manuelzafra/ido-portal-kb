# The Paradox of Influence and Diminishing Audiences

## Definition
This concept explores the inverse relationship between the depth of a practice and the size of its audience. As a practitioner delves deeper into a subject, pushing boundaries and challenging conventions, the work becomes increasingly specialized and inaccessible, leading to a smaller, more discerning audience.

## Explanation
The speaker acknowledges a shrinking audience as a natural consequence of their deepening practice. This isn’t a failure, but a necessary trade-off for pursuing a more profound understanding. Having pioneered certain approaches (e.g., bringing gymnastics and hand balancing to a wider audience), the speaker now finds themselves on the fringes, exploring territories that are too challenging or unconventional for the mainstream. This paradox highlights the inherent tension between innovation and accessibility; true advancement often requires sacrificing popularity. The speaker's willingness to embrace this diminishing audience reflects a commitment to authenticity over recognition.

## Key Insight
Genuine innovation inevitably leads to a smaller audience, as the pursuit of depth necessitates a departure from the familiar.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "I cannot hold on to the same size of crowds interested in doing ring work and handstands."
* **Outside Movement:** A groundbreaking physicist whose theories are initially rejected by the scientific community but later revolutionize the field, or a visionary artist whose work is misunderstood by contemporary critics but later celebrated as a masterpiece.

## Notes
This concept challenges the conventional wisdom that success is measured by popularity or reach. It suggests that true impact often comes from serving a smaller, more engaged audience.

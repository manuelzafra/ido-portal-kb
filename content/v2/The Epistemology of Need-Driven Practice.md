# The Epistemology of Need-Driven Practice

## Definition
This concept describes a mode of practice where the selection of tools and methods is dictated not by pre-existing frameworks or trends, but by a felt sense of what is *needed* in a given moment, even if that need is initially unclear or difficult to articulate. It prioritizes responsiveness over adherence to established protocols.

## Explanation
The speaker contrasts their approach with that of "collectors"—those who accumulate knowledge and techniques without a clear understanding of their purpose.  True practitioners, according to this view, are driven by a deeper understanding of what is lacking and what is required to address it. This understanding isn't intellectual; it's a visceral, felt sense that guides the selection of tools and methods, even if those tools seem incongruous at first glance (e.g., using Borges to inform movement practice).  The "immaculate conception" metaphor highlights the seemingly inexplicable nature of this process when viewed through the lens of conventional logic.  It’s a practice born not of intention, but of a response to a need.

## Key Insight
The most effective practice emerges not from what is known, but from a deep responsiveness to what is needed.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "Our most of our teachers and practitioners are collectors. So we don't know this intention of what is needed and serving what is needed."
* **Outside Movement:** A doctor who deviates from standard treatment protocols based on their intuition about a patient’s unique needs, or a musician who improvises a melody based on the emotional atmosphere of a performance.

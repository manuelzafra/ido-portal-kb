# The Practitioner as Curator of Practices

## Definition
This concept describes the role of a skilled practitioner not as an originator of techniques, but as a curator and synthesizer of existing practices from diverse fields, adapting them to serve a specific need or intention. They are less concerned with novelty and more focused on the underlying principles that make a practice effective.

## Explanation
The speaker positions themselves not as an innovator, but as someone who "brings" and "uses" existing practices, like Borges and Dostoevsky. This reframes the role of the teacher from a creator to a facilitator – someone who understands the underlying principles of various practices and can apply them to achieve a desired outcome. It suggests a humility in the face of accumulated knowledge, recognizing that true mastery lies not in inventing something new, but in understanding and applying what already exists. This aligns with the concept of *mimesis* in Aristotelian philosophy – learning through imitation and adaptation. The focus shifts from the "what" of a practice to the "why" – the intention and need it serves.

## Key Insight
A true practitioner’s value lies not in originality, but in the discerning application of existing practices to address specific needs.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** Using Borges and Dostoevsky as "practices" to unpack movement.
* **Outside Movement:** A doctor drawing on techniques from physiotherapy, acupuncture, and nutrition to create a holistic treatment plan for a patient.

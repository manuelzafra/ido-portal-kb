# Movement Complexity as the Core of Human Development

## Definition
Movement complexity refers to the ability to generate and adapt to a wide range of movement patterns, a capacity that Ido Portale argues is fundamental to human intelligence, neurological health, and overall development. It's not merely about physical strength or endurance, but about the brain's ability to orchestrate and learn increasingly intricate movement sequences.

## Explanation
Portale draws a parallel between mathematical learning and movement development, arguing that both involve progressing from simple tasks to increasingly complex ones. He contends that humans evolved as "the most complex movers on the planet," and that this capacity for movement complexity is intrinsically linked to the development of our brains and nervous systems.  This isn't just about physical prowess; it’s about the neurological stimulation that arises from navigating diverse and challenging movement scenarios. Activities like rock climbing, dance, and even martial arts, offer a richer tapestry of movement challenges than traditional exercise, fostering both physical and cognitive growth.  The lack of pursuit of movement complexity, he suggests, stems from a desire for ease and simplicity, hindering our potential for holistic development.

## Key Insight
The human brain's development is inextricably linked to the complexity of movement experiences.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A child learning to crawl, then walk, then run – each stage builds upon the previous, increasing movement complexity.
* **Outside Movement:**  A software developer learning a new programming language – they start with basic syntax and gradually tackle increasingly complex projects, expanding their problem-solving abilities.

# The Reward Loops and the Instrumentalization of the Self

## Definition
Societal validation and the pursuit of external rewards can create feedback loops that incentivize individuals to conform to narrow definitions of success, ultimately leading to a loss of authenticity and a sense of being used as a tool to fulfill external expectations.

## Explanation
The conversation explores how external validation, particularly in professional contexts, can trap individuals in a cycle of self-performance. The "reward loops" – the positive reinforcement received for adhering to specific societal expectations – reinforce behaviors that may not align with one's true values or potential. This can lead to a sense of being "instrumentalized," where an individual becomes a means to an end, a tool used to achieve someone else's goals. The lawyer's experience exemplifies this, as he was compelled to maintain a specific persona to satisfy market demands. Breaking free from these loops requires a conscious effort to question societal norms and prioritize internal values over external approval, even if it means sacrificing perceived success.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of external validation can trap individuals in a cycle of self-performance, leading to a loss of authenticity and a sense of being used as a tool to fulfill external expectations.

## Examples
* **Professional Life:** A musician who feels compelled to create music that conforms to popular trends, even if it doesn't resonate with their artistic vision.
* **Social Media:** An individual who alters their appearance or behavior to gain likes and followers, sacrificing authenticity for online validation.
* **Outside Movement:** A student who memorizes facts for a test, rather than genuinely engaging with the material and developing a deeper understanding.

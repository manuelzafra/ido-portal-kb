# The Interconnectedness of Internal and External Movement

## Definition
The understanding that our experience of movement is not solely about the physical body, but a complex interplay between internal sensations, external stimuli, and the environment, requiring a constant differentiation between "me" and "not me."

## Explanation
Feldenkrais’s three-element model—nervous system, mechanical system (muscle/skeleton), and environment—highlights the holistic nature of movement.  It’s not just about muscles contracting; it’s about the nervous system *receiving* information, both from within (proprioception, emotions) and from the external world. The early childhood process of differentiating “me” and “not me” is crucial for self-awareness, and movement practice provides a unique opportunity to revisit and refine this distinction. Recognizing the interplay between internal states (emotions, thoughts) and external sensations (touch, spatial awareness) fosters a deeper understanding of the self and the world. This concept has implications for emotional regulation, as it suggests that external movement can influence internal states, and vice versa.

## Key Insight
Our experience of movement is a dynamic dialogue between our internal world and the external environment, constantly shaping our sense of self.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Noticing how a change in posture (e.g., slumping vs. standing tall) affects mood and energy levels.
* **Emotional Regulation:** Using rhythmic breathing or gentle rocking to soothe anxiety by influencing the nervous system.
* **Social Interaction:** Recognizing how physical proximity and touch impact emotional responses and communication.

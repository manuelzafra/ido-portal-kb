# The Metamorphosis of Practice: From Cleaning to Weaving

## Definition
This concept describes the evolution of practice beyond mere correction or remediation (like cleaning a stain) to a transformative process that actively builds and develops the individual (like weaving a fabric). It suggests that true practice isn't just about fixing what's broken, but about creating something new and more robust.

## Explanation
The analogy of the washing machine is used to illustrate the progression of practice. Initially, it's seen as a tool to remove imperfections – a stain on a shirt. However, Ido Portal expands this to suggest that a truly transformative practice goes beyond cleaning; it actively *weaves* the fabric of the individual, creating something stronger and more complex than what existed before. This speaks to a deeper understanding of growth, suggesting that challenges and imperfections aren't simply obstacles to be overcome, but opportunities for profound development.  It aligns with the concept of "productive suffering" – the idea that difficult experiences can be catalysts for growth and transformation. This framework can be applied to any area of life, from personal relationships to professional development.

## Key Insight
True practice is not merely about fixing flaws, but about actively building and transforming oneself into something greater.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "The washing machine is not just a cleaner process of the current shirt. It develops the shirt. The shirt comes out of the washing machine, a sweatshirt."
* **External Example:** Learning a musical instrument – initially focused on correcting mistakes, eventually leading to the creation of original music and a deeper understanding of musicality.

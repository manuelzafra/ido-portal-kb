# The Journey as a Commitment to Perpetual Inquiry

## Definition
This concept frames personal development, particularly within the context of movement practices, not as a pursuit of fixed goals, but as an ongoing commitment to exploration, experimentation, and a deepening understanding of oneself and the world. It emphasizes the process over the outcome.

## Explanation
Speaker 00’s statement, "get more and more committed to the journey, even if it's not in relation to these traditional goals," encapsulates this idea. It suggests that the value lies not in achieving a specific physical or mental state, but in the continuous process of inquiry and adaptation. The Namba walk, and similar practices, become vehicles for this exploration, providing a framework for observing and responding to subtle changes in oneself and the environment. This aligns with a growth mindset, where challenges are seen as opportunities for learning and development, rather than obstacles to be overcome.

## Key Insight
The most profound growth arises not from achieving predetermined goals, but from embracing a lifelong commitment to exploration and self-discovery.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A practitioner continually experimenting with different movement variations to discover new insights.
* **Outside Movement:** A scientist dedicating their career to ongoing research, even without a clear endpoint in sight.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Growth Mindset]], [[Process-Oriented Thinking]]
* Opposes: [[Goal-Oriented Perfectionism]]

## Notes
This concept can be challenging for those accustomed to setting and achieving concrete goals. It requires a shift in perspective, from a focus on outcomes to a focus on the process of learning and growth.

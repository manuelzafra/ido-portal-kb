# The Practitioner as Synthesizer, Not Originator

## Definition
This concept describes a shift in understanding the role of a practitioner from being an originator of new techniques or styles to being a synthesizer and interpreter of existing knowledge, drawing from diverse sources to create a unique and personalized practice.

## Explanation
The speaker explicitly rejects the notion of striving for originality or uniqueness. Instead, they position themselves as a "synthesizer," actively drawing from existing sources like Borges and Dostoevsky to inform their practice. This isn't about plagiarism or imitation; it's about recognizing that all knowledge builds upon what came before. The value lies not in inventing something entirely new, but in creatively combining and recontextualizing existing elements to serve a specific purpose – the "higher thing." This perspective challenges the conventional emphasis on innovation and highlights the power of integration and adaptation.

## Key Insight
True mastery lies not in creating something entirely new, but in skillfully integrating and recontextualizing existing knowledge.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "I don't try to be unique, to be original. This is not the point… Borges is not just an author. Borges represents a practice, another mode of practice."
* **Outside Movement:** A chef who combines culinary traditions from different cultures to create a fusion cuisine.

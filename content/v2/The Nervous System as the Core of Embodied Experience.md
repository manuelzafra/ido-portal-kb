# The Nervous System as the Core of Embodied Experience

## Definition
The understanding that the nervous system is not merely a control center but the foundational element that integrates sensory input, mechanical function, and environmental interaction, shaping our perception of self and other.

## Explanation
Moshe Feldenkrais’s framework, as articulated by Portal, positions the nervous system as the "core" of embodied experience, encompassing the mechanical (muscle/skeleton) and environmental aspects. This perspective emphasizes the nervous system's role in differentiating "me" from "not me" – a crucial developmental process.  It highlights that our perception of our bodies and the world around us is actively constructed by the nervous system, constantly processing information and adjusting our responses. Recognizing this dynamic interplay allows for a deeper appreciation of how our physical sensations, thoughts, and emotions are inextricably linked.  This framework has implications for therapeutic interventions, suggesting that addressing nervous system regulation can be key to resolving physical and emotional challenges.

## Key Insight
Our sense of self is fundamentally shaped by the nervous system's ongoing integration of internal and external information.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Understanding that a feeling of stiffness isn't just a physical limitation, but a manifestation of nervous system tension.
* **Life:** Recognizing that our emotional reactions are often rooted in deeply ingrained patterns of nervous system response.

# The Paradox of Audience and Depth

## Definition
This concept describes the inverse relationship between the depth of exploration and the size of the audience that can meaningfully engage with it. As one delves deeper into a subject, the audience inevitably shrinks, creating a paradox where profound understanding is often inaccessible to the masses.

## Explanation
The speaker acknowledges that their audience is "becoming smaller and smaller" as their work becomes more profound. This isn't a failure but an inevitable consequence of pursuing depth. The initial popularity gained from introducing gymnastics and handstands was a stepping stone, but it also represented a form of limitation. To move beyond that, to truly develop, requires a willingness to sacrifice broad appeal for deeper engagement. This paradox highlights the inherent tension between accessibility and authenticity – the more authentic one's work, the less likely it is to be universally understood or appreciated. The speaker's acceptance of this shrinking audience demonstrates a commitment to their own development and the "higher thing" above the desire for external validation.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of profound understanding often necessitates a willingness to relinquish the comfort of a large audience.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A movement teacher who moves beyond basic exercises to explore complex biomechanics and philosophical underpinnings will likely attract a smaller, more dedicated following.
* **Outside Movement:** A theoretical physicist working on string theory will have a far smaller audience than a popular science writer.

## Notes
This paradox isn't necessarily negative. A smaller, more engaged audience can foster a deeper sense of community and shared purpose.

# The Epistemology of Sensory Transmission

## Definition
This concept refers to the idea that knowledge and understanding can be transmitted and absorbed through non-verbal, embodied experiences, bypassing conscious cognitive processing.

## Explanation
The speaker's assertion that "your nervous system is going to steal that information without even your cognitive understanding" reveals a profound insight into how we learn. It suggests that knowledge isn't solely acquired through verbal instruction or intellectual comprehension; it can be transmitted through physical contact and embodied demonstration. The act of "touching" a student, even with a simple tap on the back, or allowing them to place their hands on the instructor’s body, facilitates a direct transfer of information that transcends language. This highlights the limitations of purely verbal instruction and underscores the importance of embodied learning and mentorship. This concept aligns with theories of embodied cognition and the role of interoception in learning.

## Key Insight
True understanding often transcends verbal communication, and can be transmitted through direct, embodied experience.

## Examples
* **Text:** The speaker’s practice of touching students and allowing them to touch him during movement instruction.
* **Outside:** A skilled chef teaching a novice by demonstrating techniques rather than simply explaining them.

## Notes
This concept is often overlooked because it challenges the dominant paradigm of knowledge acquisition as a purely cognitive process. It requires a shift in perspective to appreciate the power of embodied learning and the limitations of verbal communication.

# The Paradox of Progress: Necessary Limitations and "Useful" Errors

## Definition
The understanding that progress often arises from embracing limitations, even those that initially appear as failures or setbacks.  "Useful" errors, or temporary limitations, can be catalysts for growth and adaptation.

## Explanation
Portell illustrates this with the example of the "perfect squat" and the evolution of movement techniques. What is considered optimal changes over time, and what might be beneficial in one context can be detrimental in another. He highlights how even seemingly "wrong" approaches can lead to unexpected breakthroughs. This concept aligns with the idea that constraints can foster creativity and innovation. The challenges imposed by limitations force us to find new solutions and develop a deeper understanding of the underlying principles.  The "flat foot squat" example demonstrates that even something initially promoted as ideal can be re-evaluated and adapted based on new information and individual needs.

## Key Insight
True progress isn't about eliminating limitations, but about understanding and leveraging them to unlock new possibilities.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "The perfect squat is easily conceptualized. For example, we used to say push the knee out, but then the Chinese came and 12 year old girls out squatted our champions with knees in."
* **Outside Movement:** A software developer who initially struggles with a particular coding language but ultimately develops a unique and innovative solution by working around its limitations.

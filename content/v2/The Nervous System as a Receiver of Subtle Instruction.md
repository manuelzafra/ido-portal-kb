# The Nervous System as a Receiver of Subtle Instruction

## Definition
This concept highlights the body’s capacity to absorb and integrate information beyond conscious understanding, suggesting that physical instruction can be transmitted through subtle cues like touch and shared movement.

## Explanation
The speaker introduces a profound insight: the nervous system can learn through non-verbal communication, even without conscious awareness.  The act of physically touching a student, even with a simple tap on the back, or having them place their hands on the instructor’s body during movement, facilitates a deeper level of learning than verbal instruction alone. This underscores the importance of embodied knowledge and the limitations of purely cognitive approaches to movement. It suggests that the body possesses an innate capacity to understand and replicate movement patterns, and that skilled instructors can leverage this capacity to facilitate learning in a more profound and efficient way.

## Key Insight
Physical instruction transcends verbal communication; the body can learn through subtle cues and shared movement experiences.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** The speaker’s statement that “your nervous system is going to steal that information without even your cognitive understanding.”
* **Outside Example:** A child learning to imitate a parent’s actions through observation and subtle physical cues, without necessarily understanding the underlying principles.

# The Paradox of Progress: Destroying to Create

## Definition
The observation that pursuing a goal or practice can ultimately lead to a sense of dissatisfaction or even obsolescence, requiring a willingness to abandon or transform what has been built.

## Explanation
Ido’s statement, "You start from art, you can finish as a banker. Start as a banker, you finish as an artist," encapsulates a profound paradox of progress.  The very act of mastering a skill or pursuing a path can lead to a sense of emptiness or a realization that the initial motivation has faded. This isn't necessarily a failure; it's a natural consequence of growth and evolution. The idea that "you destroy certain things for yourself by pursuing them" suggests that progress often requires letting go of what we've created, even if it represents significant effort and investment. This aligns with the concept of "creative destruction," where innovation inevitably disrupts and replaces existing systems.  The analogy of Sisyphus, eternally pushing a boulder uphill, highlights the cyclical nature of progress and the acceptance of ongoing effort.

## Key Insight
True progress often necessitates dismantling what has been built to create anew.

## Examples
* **Text:** "You start from art, you can finish as a banker. Start as a banker, you finish as an artist."
* **Outside:** A company disrupting its own market by developing a new technology that renders its existing products obsolete. A musician abandoning a familiar genre to explore new sonic territories.

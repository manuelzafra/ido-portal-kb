# The Collector vs. The Servant

## Definition
This concept contrasts two approaches to practice: the "collector," who amasses knowledge and techniques for personal gain or recognition, and the "servant," who uses practice to address a perceived need or deficiency.

## Explanation
The speaker identifies a common trap for teachers and practitioners: becoming "collectors" – accumulating skills and knowledge primarily for their own validation or to impress others. This approach, while seemingly productive, misses the crucial element of intentionality. A true practitioner, a "servant," is driven by a deep understanding of what is *needed* and strives to fulfill that need, even if it means sacrificing popularity or recognition. The "immaculate conception" metaphor describes the collector's approach – a seemingly miraculous display of skill that lacks a clear purpose or origin. The servant’s practice, in contrast, is grounded in a clear understanding of its purpose, making its origins and evolution transparent.

## Key Insight
The value of a practice lies not in its accumulation, but in its ability to serve a deeper need.

## Examples
* **Textual:** The distinction between teachers who focus on demonstrating impressive feats and those who use movement to address specific physical or emotional needs.
* **Outside Movement:** A chef who meticulously collects recipes and techniques but fails to create truly satisfying meals, versus a chef who focuses on understanding the ingredients and the diners’ needs.

## Notes
The distinction isn't necessarily binary; practitioners can embody elements of both the collector and the servant. The key is to be aware of the potential for the collector mentality to overshadow the servant's intention.

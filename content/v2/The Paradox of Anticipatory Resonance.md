# The Paradox of Anticipatory Resonance

## Definition
This concept describes the phenomenon where ideas initially dismissed or misunderstood eventually find resonance with a smaller, more receptive audience over time, often fulfilling a prophecy made by the originator. It highlights the delayed impact of radical ideas and the importance of maintaining a consistent message despite initial rejection.

## Explanation
The speaker's earlier predictions of a shrinking audience and the gradual acceptance of their unconventional teachings are presented as a self-fulfilling prophecy. The initial dismissal ("it, as you said, enters one ear and exits the other") is a necessary stage in the process. This isn't about ego or validation; it's about recognizing that transformative ideas often require a period of incubation before they can be fully grasped. The "clinging" to the message, despite its initial rejection, is crucial for those who are ready to receive it later. This resonates with the broader pattern of paradigm shifts, where revolutionary concepts are initially met with resistance before eventually reshaping the landscape.

## Key Insight
The most impactful ideas are often those that are initially rejected, eventually finding resonance with a receptive audience over time.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "Yeah, remember you talked about it 15 years ago in your interviews that at some point you will go smaller and smaller and smaller."
* **Outside Movement:** The initial rejection of Einstein’s theory of relativity, which was later widely accepted and fundamentally changed our understanding of physics.

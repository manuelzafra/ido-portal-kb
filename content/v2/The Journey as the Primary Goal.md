# The Journey as the Primary Goal

## Definition
This concept prioritizes the process of exploration and commitment to a path ("the journey") over the attainment of specific, pre-defined outcomes or goals ("traditional goals"). It suggests that the inherent value lies in the continuous engagement with a practice, regardless of whether it leads to a measurable result.

## Explanation
Speaker 00 emphasizes the importance of “getting more and more committed to the journey, even if it's not in relation to these traditional goals.” This is a rejection of a purely instrumental approach to practice. It implies that the pursuit of a goal can become a distraction from the deeper learning and growth that occurs through the process itself. This aligns with philosophies that emphasize the intrinsic value of action and experience, rather than solely focusing on external validation or achievement. It’s a shift from a goal-oriented mindset to a process-oriented one.

## Key Insight
The true reward lies not in reaching a destination, but in the continuous engagement with the path itself.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A practitioner focusing on the quality of movement and exploration, rather than solely on achieving a specific pose.
* **Relationships:** Prioritizing the cultivation of connection and understanding, rather than solely on achieving a specific relationship outcome.
* **Learning:** Embracing the challenges and uncertainties of learning, rather than solely on achieving a specific grade or certification.

## Notes
This concept can be challenging for those deeply ingrained in a goal-oriented culture. It requires a shift in perspective and a willingness to find value in the process itself, even when progress is not immediately apparent.

# The Value of Skills Beyond Retention: Expanding the Model of the World

## Definition
The idea that even skills and knowledge that are ultimately forgotten can contribute to personal growth by expanding one's understanding of the world and developing cognitive flexibility.

## Explanation
One speaker challenges the notion that learning something is "useless" if it's not retained. They argue that the act of learning itself—the process of expanding one's "model of the world"—is inherently valuable. Each new skill, even if later discarded, adds complexity and nuance to one's understanding, making the overall picture clearer. This perspective reframes the purpose of learning, shifting the focus from accumulation to exploration and broadening one's perspective. It acknowledges that the journey of learning is often more important than the destination of mastery.

## Key Insight
The act of learning, regardless of retention, expands our understanding of the world and fosters cognitive flexibility, contributing to personal growth.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Learning a complex dance routine, even if it’s not memorized, can improve coordination, rhythm, and spatial awareness.
* **Life:** Studying a historical period, even if the details are forgotten, can broaden one’s perspective on human behavior and societal development.

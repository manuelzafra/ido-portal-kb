# The Distinction Between "Doing" and "Being" in Practice

## Definition
This concept differentiates between the conventional understanding of "doing" – actively pursuing goals and achieving outcomes – and "being" in practice – a state of embodied presence and responsiveness that transcends intentional action. It suggests that true growth arises not from striving, but from cultivating a deeper connection to oneself and the world.

## Explanation
Portal argues that many people mistakenly equate "practice" with deliberate action and performance. However, he posits that true practice involves a shift from *doing* to *being* – a state of receptivity and responsiveness where action arises organically from a deeper connection to oneself and the environment. This isn't about passivity, but about relinquishing the need to control outcomes and allowing the practice itself to guide the process. It’s about moving beyond the illusion of control and embracing the inherent uncertainty of life. This resonates with Zen Buddhist principles of "wu wei" – effortless action – and the importance of cultivating a state of mindful presence. The emphasis is on the process of becoming, rather than the achievement of a specific goal.

## Key Insight
True growth emerges not from striving, but from cultivating a state of embodied presence and responsiveness that transcends intentional action.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "So for me, that is what I'm talking about, that is what I'm sharing, that is what I'm teaching, how to evolve ourselves, to grow ourselves by empowering the practice beyond ourselves."
* **Outside Example:** A skilled improvisational jazz musician doesn't consciously plan their solos; they respond to the music and the energy of the moment, allowing the music to flow through them.

## Notes
This concept can be challenging to grasp because it requires a shift in perspective – moving away from a goal-oriented mindset towards a more process-oriented one. It's not about abandoning action, but about transforming the *way* we act.

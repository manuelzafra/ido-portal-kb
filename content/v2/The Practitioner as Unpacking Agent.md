# The Practitioner as Unpacking Agent

## Definition
The role of a practitioner transcends mere execution; it becomes a process of "unpacking" – revealing the underlying principles and structures embedded within a system or tradition. This unpacking isn't about deconstruction, but about illuminating the inherent intelligence of the practice.

## Explanation
The speaker describes practitioners, particularly those who engage with texts like Borges, as "unpacking agents." This goes beyond simply understanding the surface level of a practice; it involves revealing the hidden logic, the underlying assumptions, and the deeper connections that make it work. This unpacking is not a critical dissection, but a process of revelation – making visible the inherent intelligence and structure that already exists within the practice. It’s about understanding *how* something works, not just *what* it is, and then using that understanding to inform and refine one's own practice.  This requires a willingness to look beyond the obvious and to engage with the practice on a deeper, more intuitive level.

## Key Insight
A true practitioner doesn't just perform a practice; they understand and reveal its underlying structure.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "And then naturally I used it...it gives another mode of unpacking."
* **Outside Movement:** A chef who understands the chemical reactions behind cooking, allowing them to innovate and adapt recipes; a musician who understands the harmonic principles of music, enabling them to improvise and compose.

## Notes
The term "unpacking" is crucial here. It implies a gentle revelation, not a forceful dismantling. It's about bringing something into clearer light, not destroying it.

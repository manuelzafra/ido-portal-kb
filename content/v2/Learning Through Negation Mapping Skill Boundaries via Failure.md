# Learning Through Negation: Mapping Skill Boundaries via Failure

## Definition
This pedagogical approach involves intentionally introducing failure as a crucial step in skill acquisition. By experiencing and analyzing what *doesn't* work, learners can more effectively map the boundaries of successful execution and refine their technique.

## Explanation
The speaker explicitly states, "Learning through negation...First, the failure is experienced, then we can go for the actual performance." This isn't simply about trial and error; it's a deliberate strategy to define the limits of a skill. By first exploring what leads to failure – a missed box jump, a dropped snatch – the learner gains a clearer understanding of the parameters within which success is possible. This process creates a "map" of the skill, delineating the safe and effective pathways.  This framework is analogous to scientific inquiry, where hypotheses are tested and refined through observation of negative results. It's a powerful method for building competence and confidence by confronting and overcoming challenges.

## Key Insight
True understanding of a skill emerges not just from successes, but from the careful analysis of failures.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "Once you go through break falling 101, we start flipping."
* **Outside Example:** A child learning to ride a bike – falling is an inevitable and essential part of the process.

# The Re-Enchantment of the Mundane Through Play

## Definition
This concept describes the process of rediscovering joy and learning through playful engagement with everyday objects and environments, reclaiming a childlike perspective that is often lost in adulthood.

## Explanation
Portal's emphasis on using simple tools like broomsticks and encouraging movements like crawling like a lizard reveals a philosophy of re-enchantment.  Adults often compartmentalize experiences, labeling certain activities as "serious" and others as "play."  This artificial separation stifles creativity and limits potential. By encouraging playful interaction with the mundane – a broomstick, the floor, gravity – Portal invites participants to reconnect with a more primal, exploratory mode of being. This echoes the work of philosophers like Johan Huizinga, who argued that play is fundamental to culture and learning.  The act of crawling, for instance, isn’t just a physical exercise; it’s a symbolic reclaiming of a lost connection to the earth and a rejection of the rigid structures imposed by adult expectations. It's a reminder that learning and growth can be found in the simplest of interactions.

## Key Insight
True learning and growth often arise not from complex instruction, but from playful exploration and re-engagement with the world through a childlike sense of wonder.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A child effortlessly explores movement possibilities with a stick, whereas an adult might see it only as a cleaning tool.
* **Outside Movement:** A child building a fort from blankets and pillows demonstrates a creativity and problem-solving ability that can be stifled by structured activities.

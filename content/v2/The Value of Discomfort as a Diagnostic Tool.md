# The Value of Discomfort as a Diagnostic Tool

## Definition
The speaker proposes that discomfort and tension, even when seemingly negative, can be valuable sources of information about the body's state and underlying imbalances.

## Explanation
The practice of lying on the ground and examining contact points isn't about stretching or manipulating the body, but about gathering diagnostic information. Discomfort isn't something to be avoided, but rather a signal to be investigated. This aligns with a holistic approach to health and movement, where pain and tension are viewed as messages from the body, rather than simply obstacles to be overcome.  The speaker’s emphasis on “what we note, what we pay attention to, we get to keep” highlights the importance of mindful observation in unlocking this diagnostic potential. This connects to the broader concept of biofeedback, where individuals learn to interpret and respond to physiological signals.

## Key Insight
Discomfort and tension can be valuable diagnostic tools, providing insights into the body's underlying state.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "Of course in the beginning you don't pay attention and you don't lay much on the ground. So you're not aware of it. But after a while it starts to become very good diagnostic."
* **Outside Example:** A skilled mechanic doesn't ignore unusual noises from an engine; they listen carefully to diagnose the underlying problem.

## Notes
This concept requires a shift in mindset from viewing discomfort as something to be eliminated to seeing it as a valuable source of information. It encourages a more attentive and exploratory approach to movement and body awareness.

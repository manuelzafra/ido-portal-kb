# The Erosion of Intuition: The Cost of Over-Protection

## Definition
The idea that excessive protection and intervention in childhood – such as restricting movement or correcting posture – can stifle a child's natural, intuitive movement patterns and ultimately lead to physical limitations and dysfunction.

## Explanation
[SPEAKER_00] expresses frustration with parents and therapists who restrict children's natural movements, citing the example of a child being told not to sit in a "W" position on the floor. This intervention, intended to be protective, actually disrupts the child's innate understanding of their body and its capabilities. The speaker argues that this over-protection leads to a loss of mobility, increased susceptibility to injury, and a dependence on external guidance. This framework connects to the concept of embodied cognition – the idea that our thinking and understanding are deeply rooted in our physical experiences.  The speaker’s lament about children being “locked in chairs” by age six highlights the long-term consequences of this interference.

## Key Insight
Interfering with a child's natural movement patterns, even with good intentions, can inadvertently damage their physical development and limit their potential.

## Examples
* **Text:** "You are destroying it [the child's natural movement system]."
* **Outside:**  Overly structured parenting styles that stifle a child's creativity and independence.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Embodied Cognition]], [[Nature vs. Nurture]]
* Opposes: [[Helicopter Parenting]], [[Over-Correction]]
* Deeper pattern: [[The Butterfly Effect]] (small changes in initial conditions can have significant consequences later on)

## Notes
This concept is easily misunderstood as advocating for a completely laissez-faire approach to parenting. The speaker is not suggesting that children should be left to their own devices, but rather that their natural movement patterns should be respected and encouraged, rather than corrected.

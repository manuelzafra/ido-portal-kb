# The Diagnostic Field of Movement

## Definition
This concept describes a pedagogical approach where the instructor doesn't directly instruct but instead creates a "diagnostic field"—a scenario or challenge—to reveal a student's underlying limitations and strengths. The student's response to this challenge then informs the instructor's subsequent guidance.

## Explanation
The speaker contrasts traditional instruction ("follow me, one, two, three") with a more nuanced approach that prioritizes observation and self-discovery. Instead of dictating movement, he creates situations—like placing a knee on a student's stomach or asking them to sing while practicing—to expose their vulnerabilities and reveal their innate capabilities. The student's reaction to these scenarios becomes the primary source of information, guiding the instructor's interventions. This isn't about punishment or discomfort; it's about creating a space for self-awareness and facilitating a deeper understanding of one's own body and movement patterns. This approach emphasizes the student's agency and fosters a more organic learning process.

## Key Insight
True learning emerges not from direct instruction, but from observing and responding to the challenges we create for ourselves.

## Examples
* **Text:** "I put my knee on your stomach in a top position and you allow it, we create this scenario and now you have to manage yourself under this pressure and find a way to breathe."
* **Outside:** A manager might create a challenging project for a team member, not to test their skills, but to observe their problem-solving approach and identify areas for development. A therapist might present a patient with a difficult situation to understand their coping mechanisms.

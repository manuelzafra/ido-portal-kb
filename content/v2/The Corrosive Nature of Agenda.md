# The Corrosive Nature of Agenda

## Definition
This concept describes how a pre-existing need for validation or self-definition – an "agenda" – fundamentally distorts perception and hinders genuine understanding. The drive to prove oneself or achieve a specific outcome creates a filter that prevents unbiased observation and leads to a proliferation of falsehoods and difficulties.

## Explanation
The speaker argues that a lack of inherent self-worth compels individuals to seek external validation, leading them to approach practices with a hidden agenda. This agenda, whether conscious or unconscious, biases their perception and prevents them from seeing the truth. The desire to prove something, to fit into a pre-defined narrative, generates a distorted reality. Conversely, a sense of inherent worth allows for a playful, exploratory approach, free from the need to justify one's existence. This aligns with existentialist thought, which emphasizes the importance of authentic being and the rejection of externally imposed definitions.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of external validation through practice inevitably obscures the very understanding one seeks.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "But if I don't have that sense of worth, I'm already like geared towards I need to do this, I need to prove this, I have this agenda."
* **Outside Example:** A politician crafting a speech not to inform, but to secure votes, is driven by an agenda that compromises the integrity of their message.

## Notes
This isn’t a condemnation of ambition, but a warning about the corrupting influence of *need*. Genuine growth arises from a place of abundance, not scarcity.

# The Dualistic Nature of Inner and Outer Stillness

## Definition
True stillness and inner peace are not solely achieved through external practices like meditation or quiet contemplation, but also through embracing a dynamic, engaged existence that incorporates both stillness and movement.  The absence of external activity can mask internal turmoil, while external activity can be a form of internal stillness.

## Explanation
McGregor’s description of his practice, encompassing both seated stillness and dynamic movement like Sufi dances, reveals a nuanced understanding of stillness. He highlights that stillness can manifest outwardly through physical postures or inwardly through mental clarity. Conversely, movement can be a form of meditation, a way to process emotions and find equilibrium. This dualistic perspective challenges the conventional notion of stillness as merely the absence of activity, suggesting that it's a state of balance and integration between internal and external experiences.  It’s a recognition that true stillness isn't about escaping the world, but about navigating it with awareness and presence.

## Key Insight
Genuine stillness is not the absence of movement, but a harmonious integration of inner peace and dynamic engagement with the world.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** McGregor’s comparison of external stillness (seated meditation) and internal stillness (Sufi dances).
* **Outside Example:** A chef who finds peace and focus through the rhythmic, repetitive motions of cooking, even amidst a busy kitchen.

## Notes

# The "Trojan Horse" of Instruction

## Definition
Subtle, seemingly innocuous instructions can be used to subtly challenge ingrained habits and push individuals beyond their comfort zones, creating a learning experience that is both engaging and transformative.

## Explanation
The instructor intentionally introduces instructions like "right" or "left" during the stick-catching exercise, knowing that participants will likely react automatically. This creates a "Trojan horse" scenario, where a simple instruction masks a deeper challenge to their reflexes and decision-making processes. By observing how individuals respond to these subtle cues, the instructor gains valuable insight into their cognitive and movement patterns. This technique highlights the power of indirect instruction and the importance of creating learning experiences that are both playful and challenging. It connects to pedagogical approaches that emphasize discovery and experiential learning.

## Key Insight
The most effective instruction isn't always direct; sometimes, it's a subtle challenge disguised as a simple command, forcing the learner to adapt and discover new capabilities.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "I played a little game with him. I put a little Trojan horse in there. So I gave him right and left but towards the end I said light. not right. Maybe he thought it was my accent."
* **Outside Example:** A coach subtly encouraging a hesitant athlete to take a risk by framing it as a simple suggestion, allowing them to overcome their fear and discover their potential.

## Notes
This technique requires a deep understanding of human psychology and the ability to anticipate how individuals will react to subtle cues. It's also important to avoid being manipulative; the intention should be to facilitate learning, not to deceive.

# The Teacher as Teaching

## Definition
A shift in perspective where the teacher transcends the role of instructor and becomes a living embodiment of the principles they impart, demonstrating those principles through their actions and being, rather than solely through verbal instruction.

## Explanation
The speaker's statement, "I don't want to be a teacher. I want to be the teaching," represents a profound redefinition of the teacher's role. It moves beyond the traditional model of imparting knowledge to a model of embodying the desired qualities and behaviors. This means that students learn not just from what the teacher *says*, but from *who* the teacher is – their actions, their responses to challenges, their overall demeanor. This approach emphasizes the importance of authenticity and self-awareness for teachers, as their imperfections and vulnerabilities become integral parts of the learning process. It also highlights the power of observation and imitation in learning, suggesting that students often learn more from what they *see* than from what they *hear*.

## Key Insight
The most effective teaching isn't about conveying information, but about embodying the principles you wish to impart.

## Examples
* **Movement:** A dance instructor who not only teaches steps but also demonstrates the artistry and emotional expression inherent in the dance.
* **Life:** A parent who models honesty, resilience, and empathy through their actions.
* **Learning:** A mentor who shares their experiences, both successes and failures, to guide a mentee's development.

## Notes
This approach requires a high degree of self-awareness and a willingness to be vulnerable, as the teacher's actions are constantly under observation. It also places a greater responsibility on the teacher to live a life that aligns with their teachings.

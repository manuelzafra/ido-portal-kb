# The Inevitable Disappointment of Achieving Goals

## Definition
This concept explores the paradoxical nature of achieving goals, suggesting that the satisfaction derived from accomplishment is often fleeting and can even lead to a sense of existential despair. It challenges the conventional wisdom that success equates to fulfillment and highlights the importance of embracing a process-oriented approach to life.

## Explanation
Portal observes that those who achieve their goals often find themselves facing a void, a realization that the pursuit itself was more meaningful than the attainment. This isn't a condemnation of ambition, but a recognition that external validation and achievement are ultimately insufficient for lasting fulfillment. The "rat race" of chasing goals becomes exposed upon reaching them, revealing a cycle of striving and disappointment. This aligns with Buddhist principles of impermanence and the futility of clinging to external outcomes. It suggests that true fulfillment lies not in reaching a destination, but in the journey itself, in the continuous process of growth and exploration.  The focus shifts from *what* is achieved to *how* one engages with the process of becoming.

## Key Insight
The pursuit of goals, while seemingly desirable, can lead to disappointment when the attainment reveals the emptiness of external validation.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "But then what happens when you reach your goals? Well, Fred, I'm sure someone like you already discovered, because those who actually reach their goals discover that the rat race of of running after these goals is exposed in the moment of reaching them."
* **Outside Example:** A writer who finally publishes their dream novel might find that the experience is less euphoric than anticipated, leading them to question their career choices and seek new challenges.

This concept can be unsettling for those deeply invested in goal-oriented thinking. It's not meant to discourage ambition, but to encourage a more nuanced understanding of its limitations and to cultivate a deeper appreciation for the journey itself.

# The Practice as a Mode of Unpacking

## Definition
This concept proposes that certain texts and artistic works are not merely sources of information or inspiration, but serve as structured methods for understanding and engaging with the world – "practices" in their own right.

## Explanation
The speaker doesn't view Borges or Dostoevsky as authors in the conventional sense; they are "practices," "another mode of practice." This implies that these works contain inherent structures and approaches to thinking that can be actively employed. Reading and working through these texts isn’t about passive consumption but about engaging in a specific method of unpacking – a way of deconstructing assumptions, revealing hidden layers of meaning, and ultimately, understanding the world in a new way. This elevates the role of the reader/practitioner from consumer to active participant in a process of discovery. It suggests that certain artistic creations possess a pedagogical quality, capable of shaping one's cognitive framework.

## Key Insight
Certain works of art can function as structured practices, offering a unique method for understanding and engaging with the world.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** "And those texts, when you are a practitioner, it was very clear to me. This is a practice. This is another mode of practice."
* **Outside Movement:** Studying a musical composition not just for its beauty, but for the underlying principles of harmony and structure that can inform one's own creative process.

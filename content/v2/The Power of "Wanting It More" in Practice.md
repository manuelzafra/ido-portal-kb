# The Power of "Wanting It More" in Practice

## Definition
This concept describes a crucial element of growth and mastery: the recognition that true progress isn't solely about inherent talent or ability, but about the relentless desire to push beyond one's perceived limitations, even when facing discomfort and perceived failure.

## Explanation
The speaker recounts experiences from his martial arts training, including being kicked by a larger instructor and enduring painful exercises. These weren't simply about physical endurance; they were about confronting the feeling of being outmatched and choosing to persevere. The phrase "he wants it more than me" becomes a catalyst for self-improvement, prompting a deeper commitment and a willingness to endure discomfort. This extends beyond physical training, representing a broader principle applicable to any endeavor. It's not about eliminating the feeling of inadequacy, but about using it as fuel to drive progress. The speaker’s father’s wrestling with him, allowing him to "give up" and then encouraging him, is a powerful illustration of this principle – creating a safe space to experience and overcome perceived limitations. This framework highlights the importance of embracing struggle and recognizing that true growth often arises from moments of perceived weakness.

## Key Insight
True mastery is not about avoiding discomfort or feeling inadequate, but about harnessing the desire to overcome those feelings and push beyond perceived limitations.

## Examples
* **Textual Example:** The speaker's experience being kicked by his martial arts instructor and the subsequent realization that he needed to "want it more."
* **Outside Example:** An athlete pushing through pain and exhaustion to achieve a personal best, driven by the desire to outperform their previous self.

## Notes
This concept is often confused with simple perseverance. It's more nuanced, emphasizing the *motivation* derived from recognizing one's limitations and the desire to surpass them.

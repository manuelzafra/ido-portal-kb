# The Nervous System's Implicit Learning

## Definition
This concept describes how the nervous system can absorb and integrate information through non-verbal, often unconscious means, such as physical touch or shared movement. It suggests that instruction isn't solely reliant on cognitive understanding.

## Explanation
The speaker describes a novel teaching method where he physically touches his students, even just with a tap on the back, and allows them to reciprocate by touching him while he moves. This goes beyond verbal instruction, recognizing that the nervous system can "steal that information without even your cognitive understanding." This highlights the limitations of purely verbal instruction and the importance of embodied learning – learning through direct physical experience. It suggests that the body possesses a deep, intuitive understanding that can be accessed through non-verbal communication.

## Key Insight
The nervous system possesses an innate capacity for learning that transcends verbal instruction, emphasizing the importance of embodied experiences and physical connection in the learning process.

## Examples
* **Text:** The speaker's description of physically touching students and allowing them to touch him during movement.
* **Outside:** A child learning to dance by mimicking their parent's movements without explicit instruction.

## Notes
This approach requires a high level of trust and rapport between the instructor and the student. It also highlights the importance of being mindful of physical boundaries and consent.

# The Paradox of High-Tech Dependence and Movement

## Definition
This concept highlights the counterintuitive relationship between technological advancement and human movement proficiency. It posits that increased reliance on specialized equipment and "high-tech" solutions often leads to a decline in fundamental, innate movement capabilities, rendering individuals more dependent and less resourceful.

## Explanation
Ido Portal's observation that "the more expensive the toys, the cheaper is the mover" encapsulates a profound paradox. While technology promises to enhance performance and alleviate limitations, it frequently creates new dependencies. The reliance on specialized footwear, gym equipment, or pre-programmed routines can atrophy the body's natural ability to adapt, problem-solve, and move efficiently. This isn't simply about physical decline; it’s about a loss of embodied intelligence and resilience. The anecdote about the Olympic rings being deemed a safety hazard underscores this point – a desire to simplify and standardize movement often stifles the very creativity and adaptability that makes human movement so remarkable. This aligns with broader philosophical critiques of technological determinism, suggesting that tools, rather than liberating us, can subtly constrain our potential if not approached with mindful awareness. The concept connects to the broader idea of "skill decay" in various domains – the more we outsource a task to a tool, the less proficient we become at performing it ourselves.

## Key Insight
True movement mastery lies not in acquiring the latest gear, but in cultivating a deep connection to one's body and embracing the inherent capabilities that technology often obscures.

## Examples
* **Text Example:** "The more expensive the toys, the cheaper is the mover."
* **Outside Example:** A musician who relies solely on digital audio workstations (DAWs) may lose the ability to improvise or play by ear, hindering their musical development.

## Notes
This concept is easily misunderstood as a rejection of technology altogether. Instead, it advocates for a balanced approach, where technology serves as a tool to enhance, not replace, fundamental movement skills.

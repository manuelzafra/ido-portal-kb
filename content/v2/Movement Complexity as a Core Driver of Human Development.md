# Movement Complexity as a Core Driver of Human Development

## Definition
The concept that the human brain and nervous system evolved primarily to manage and process increasingly complex movement patterns, and that cultivating this complexity is essential for overall development, including cognitive and emotional well-being.

## Explanation
Portale draws a parallel between mathematical learning and movement development, arguing that both require a progression from simple to complex challenges. Just as learning addition and subtraction is a foundation for calculus, mastering basic movements is a prerequisite for more sophisticated ones. He posits that human intelligence and the very structure of the brain are deeply intertwined with our ability to generate and adapt to diverse movement patterns.  This isn't merely about physical prowess; it's about the neurological pathways that are strengthened through the constant negotiation of space, balance, and coordination.  Activities like martial arts, dance, and even seemingly simple actions like walking are rich sources of movement complexity, stimulating the brain in ways that repetitive exercise often fails to do.  The core of being human, according to Portale, is our capacity for diverse and adaptable movement.

## Key Insight
The human brain’s development is inextricably linked to our ability to navigate and master increasingly complex movement patterns, making movement complexity the cornerstone of human intelligence and well-being.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Learning to juggle, requiring coordination, timing, and spatial awareness.
* **Limited by Simplification:**  Repetitive weightlifting without variation, neglecting the neurological benefits of complex movement patterns.
* **Outside Movement:**  Navigating a new city, requiring constant adaptation to unfamiliar environments and unexpected obstacles.

## Notes
The concept of "movement complexity" isn't about performing the most difficult or acrobatic feats. It's about engaging in activities that challenge your body and brain to adapt and coordinate in novel ways.

# The Interconnectedness of Body, Mind, and Emotion Through Movement

## Definition
The idea that movement is not merely a physical activity but a holistic expression encompassing the body, mind, emotions, and even life itself, and that awareness of this interconnectedness is a fundamental aspect of a meaningful practice.

## Explanation
Portal frames movement practice as "an examination and bringing this awareness into things," extending beyond the physical body to include the mind ("your mind is a type of movement") and emotions. This aligns with embodied cognition, the theory that our cognitive processes are deeply intertwined with our physical experiences.  By paying attention to the movement of emotions, one can gain insight into their underlying patterns and triggers.  This perspective suggests that physical movement can be a powerful tool for emotional regulation and self-understanding, and vice versa. It's a recognition that the body isn't a separate entity but a dynamic interface between the individual and the world.

## Key Insight
By observing the movement of the body, we can gain profound insights into the movement of the mind and emotions, revealing a deeper understanding of ourselves.

## Examples
* **Movement:** Noticing how tension in the shoulders correlates with anxiety.
* **Life:** Recognizing how a slumped posture reflects a lack of confidence.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Embodied Cognition]], [[Holism]], [[Self-Awareness]]
* Opposes: [[Dualism]] (the separation of mind and body)
* Deeper pattern: [[Interconnectedness]]

## Notes
This concept can be easily misunderstood as advocating for a purely emotional or psychological approach to movement. It emphasizes the *interconnectedness* of these aspects, not the primacy of one over the others.

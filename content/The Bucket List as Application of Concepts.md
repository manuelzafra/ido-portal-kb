# 2. The Bucket List as Application of Concepts

## Definition
This concept reframes the traditional “bucket list” – a list of desired experiences – as a tool for applying learned movement concepts, rather than a passive pursuit of external goals.

## Explanation
Instead of simply wanting to “travel the world” or “learn a new skill,” the focus shifts to *how* those experiences can be used to actively engage with and deepen understanding of movement principles. It’s about using experiences as a laboratory for applying knowledge.

## Key Insight
“The bucket list is more in relation to the application of these concepts.”

## Examples
The speaker’s examples of stopping travel, impressive movement, and social media interactions are all instances of consciously choosing to *not* engage in activities that don’t serve the core principle of applying movement concepts.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Intentionality]], [[Purposeful Action]]
*   Opposes: [[Hedonic Pursuit]]

## Notes
This highlights a shift from external validation (achieving a list of accomplishments) to internal motivation (using experiences to deepen understanding).

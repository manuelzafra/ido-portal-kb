# The “Never Leave the Playground” Methodology

## Definition
A training approach focused on continuous exploration and experimentation, regardless of immediate success or mastery, emphasizing the value of the learning process itself.

## Explanation
This methodology, championed by Stephen Jepsen, advocates for persistent engagement in movement activities, even if progress is slow or seemingly unproductive. The focus is on the sensory experience and the development of movement awareness, rather than solely on achieving specific outcomes.

## Key Insight
“Try to learn how to juggle. Whether you made it or not, two people devoted three months to the practice. One made it and can juggle three balls. The other one cannot. MRI studies of their brain will show the same exact results.” – The value of the process, not just the outcome.

## Examples
*   Spending time crawling on the floor, exploring different movement patterns, even if the goal is simply to improve mobility.
*   Engaging in playful movement activities without a specific objective, focusing on the sensory experience and the development of movement awareness.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Growth Mindset]], [[Deliberate Practice]]
*   Opposes: [[Outcome-Driven Training]]

## Notes
This concept highlights the importance of embracing a growth mindset and viewing movement training as a continuous journey of exploration and discovery.

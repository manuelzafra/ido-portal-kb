# 3. Movement as a “Driving” Skill

## Definition
Movement is not simply a physical activity but a complex, dynamic skill that requires focused attention, coordination, and control – akin to driving a vehicle.

## Explanation
This perspective reframes movement training as a cognitive process, emphasizing the need to develop a “movement sense” – the ability to anticipate, adjust, and control movement in real-time.

## Key Insight
“What are the traditional martial artists? They are garages. They fix cars, they develop pieces, shiny rods on the car, pimp my ride.” –  This metaphor highlights the need to diagnose and address movement problems with a diagnostic and corrective approach.

## Examples
*   Comparing movement to driving: “You give them any car, Toyota, and you go against them with a formula one, and they win.” – This illustrates the importance of developing a fundamental movement skill before attempting more complex techniques.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Motor Control]], [[Skill Acquisition]]
*   Opposes: [[Passive Movement]] (simply executing a movement without conscious awareness)

## Notes
*   This concept underscores the importance of developing a “movement sense” – a deep understanding of how the body moves and interacts with the environment.

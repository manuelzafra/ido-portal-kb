# The Intention-Driven Practice

## Definition
A practice driven by a deep understanding of needs and limitations, rather than by external trends, cultural norms, or the pursuit of novelty.

## Explanation
This describes a practice that isn’t about creating something “new” or “unique” for its own sake. Instead, it’s about intentionally applying knowledge and techniques to address underlying needs, acknowledging the inherent constraints of the human condition.

## Key Insight
The most effective practice arises from a clear understanding of what’s truly needed and a willingness to serve that need, regardless of external validation.

## Examples
Ido Portal’s use of Borges as a tool for unpacking movement concepts, not as a source of trendy ideas.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Layered Sensory Awareness in Movement]], [[The Practice of Unknowing]]
* Opposes: [[Collector Mentality]] (seeking novelty for its own sake)

## Notes
This concept emphasizes the importance of intention – a deliberate focus on serving a deeper purpose, rather than chasing superficial goals.

# The “Squeeze of a Line” – Focused Practice

## Definition
This refers to a highly concentrated, targeted practice approach where the practitioner focuses intensely on a single, specific movement quality or aspect of movement, pushing themselves to the edge of their current capabilities.

## Explanation
It’s not about doing a lot of movement; it’s about doing *focused* movement. The “squeeze” represents the intense pressure and concentration applied to that single element, forcing the body to adapt and refine its response. It’s a deliberate attempt to break through plateaus and unlock new levels of movement.

## Key Insight
Significant progress in movement often comes from intensely focused, targeted practice, rather than broad, unfocused repetition.

## Examples
*   Spending 15 minutes solely focusing on maintaining perfect balance on one leg, consciously adjusting the hips and core to minimize movement.
*   Repeating a single movement pattern – a specific arm swing or leg kick – with the sole intention of feeling the subtle shifts in muscle activation and timing.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Layered Sensory Awareness in Movement]] (the focused practice enhances sensory awareness)
*   Related to: [[Progressive Overload]] (the intense focus creates a stimulus for adaptation)

## Notes
This approach is often contrasted with “doing a lot of movement,” which can be inefficient and lead to burnout. It’s about quality over quantity.

# 2. The Hoarding Disease of Skills

## Definition
The tendency to retain skills superficially, holding onto them without actively utilizing them, leading to a diminished capacity to apply them effectively.

## Explanation
This describes a state of mental clutter where skills are stored as abstract concepts rather than integrated into functional movement. The individual holds onto the *idea* of the skill, but doesn’t engage in the practice that would solidify it.

## Key Insight
True skill retention requires active engagement and application, not passive storage of knowledge.

## Examples
*   “You take a rock limber, you put them in the gym and they lift a lot of weight on the machines. They might not be specialized but they can do this is a smart maintenance we should teach it to the students.” – This illustrates the unproductive habit of simply accumulating knowledge without applying it.
*   “I don't remember the last time I did a bicep curl.” – A clear example of a skill that has been superficially retained but no longer actively used.

# 1. The Nature of Practice

## Definition
“Practice” is not a fixed goal or outcome, but a flowing, unfolding process – a river you don’t enter, but recognize yourself within. It’s fundamentally about continuous transformation and becoming, not about achieving a static state.

## Explanation
This concept frames “practice” as a dynamic, process-oriented approach to life. It rejects the idea of a fixed endpoint or a “successful” state, emphasizing instead the ongoing nature of learning, adaptation, and self-discovery. It’s about being *in* the process, not just *doing* something.

## Key Insight
“Practice is life and life is practice.” – A fundamental shift in perspective, recognizing that all of existence is inherently a process of becoming.

## Examples
*   The 13-hour days and two-hour lectures during the intensive, which forced a re-evaluation of what “practice” truly meant.
*   The horse stance training – a deliberate disruption of homeostasis to reveal deeper insights.

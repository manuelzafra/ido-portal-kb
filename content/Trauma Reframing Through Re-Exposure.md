# Trauma Reframing Through Re-Exposure

## Definition
The process of reducing the emotional impact of a traumatic experience by repeatedly exposing oneself to a controlled version of the event, allowing the nervous system to re-learn and reframe the memory.

## Explanation
This concept, popularized by David Spiegel, suggests that avoidance of a traumatic trigger actually strengthens the associated fear response. Controlled re-exposure, particularly with sensory elements, can disrupt this pattern and lead to a diminished emotional reaction over time.

## Key Insight
The act of confronting a traumatic memory, even in a safe and controlled environment, can fundamentally alter its impact.

## Examples
*   The traditional practice of burning a burn wound repeatedly to prevent scar tissue formation and reduce pain.
*   Applying a controlled dose of fear in a movement practice to diminish the intensity of a traumatic memory associated with that movement.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Trauma Response]], [[Neuroplasticity]], [[Controlled Exposure Therapy]]
*   Opposes: [[Avoidance as a Trauma Response Strategy]]

## Notes
This is a specific application of principles from trauma therapy, adapted to movement contexts. It’s not about reliving the trauma, but about creating a new, less fearful association.

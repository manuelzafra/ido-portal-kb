# The "N of One" Principle

## Definition
The value of personal experimentation and observation as the primary source of knowledge, acknowledging that individual experience constitutes a limited but valuable dataset.

## Explanation
Rather than relying on aggregated data or expert opinions, each individual’s unique movement practice and resulting experience represents a crucial “N of one” – a single data point that informs personal understanding and practice.

## Key Insight
Personal experience, however limited, is the most reliable source of knowledge in the context of movement and self-discovery.

## Examples
Tracking subjective feelings, movement quality, or cognitive states during a movement practice to identify patterns and insights specific to one’s own body and mind.

## Relationships
* Related to: [[Experimentation in Learning]], [[Personalized Practice]]
* Opposes: [[Blind Faith in Authority]]

## Notes
This principle encourages a humble and iterative approach to learning, recognizing the limitations of external knowledge and prioritizing direct observation.

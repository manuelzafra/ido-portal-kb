# Letting Go of the "Perfect"

## Definition
This concept rejects the pursuit of a single, ideal execution of a movement, recognizing that movement is inherently variable and context-dependent. It’s about accepting imperfection and focusing on the process of exploration and adaptation.

## Explanation
The “perfect” squat, for example, isn’t a fixed position but a functional configuration tailored to the specific demands of the task at hand. Rigid adherence to a single “perfect” form can actually hinder movement efficiency and adaptability.

## Key Insight
Transformation and genuine progress require the willingness to relinquish fixed ideas and embrace the inherent variability of movement.

## Examples
*   The example of the Chinese 12-year-old girls outsquating champions with knees in, demonstrating that a “perfect” squat isn’t a universal standard.
*   The realization that a specific movement technique may be optimal for one situation but ineffective for another.

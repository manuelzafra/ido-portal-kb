# 7.  The Importance of "Being" vs. "Knowing"

## Definition
This concept distinguishes between acquiring knowledge of movement and actually *being* in movement, emphasizing the experiential and embodied nature of learning.

## Explanation
It’s about moving beyond simply understanding the mechanics of a movement to actually *feeling* the movement in your body, allowing it to inform your actions and decisions.

## Key Insight
“We want to become movement, which is very different from learning movement.”

## Examples
The speaker's observation that simply studying a movement doesn't guarantee that you'll be able to perform it effectively.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Embodied Cognition]]
*   Opposes: [[Cognitive Learning Alone]]

## Notes
This highlights the crucial role of experience in learning and development.

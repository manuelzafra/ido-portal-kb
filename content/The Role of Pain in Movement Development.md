# The Role of Pain in Movement Development

## Definition
The strategic introduction of controlled discomfort and pain as a stimulus for physiological adaptation and enhanced movement capabilities.

## Explanation
This approach suggests that intentionally exposing the body to small doses of pain – through exercises like wrist push-ups or restricted movements – can trigger a cascade of physiological changes that ultimately lead to improved strength, stability, and resilience.

## Key Insight
“You introduce us to a small dose of pain, I'd say, kind of Ido portal style.” –  Highlighting the deliberate use of discomfort as a training tool.

## Examples
*   Attempting wrist push-ups to challenge the stability of the wrists and shoulders.
*   Performing movements with restricted joint ranges to stimulate adaptation and improve movement efficiency.

## Relationships
*   Related to: [[Neuromuscular Control]], [[Adaptive Physiology]]
*   Opposes: [[Avoidance of Discomfort]]

## Notes
This concept emphasizes the importance of embracing discomfort as a necessary component of movement training and highlights the potential for controlled pain to drive physiological adaptation.
